# Target Market: The Autism Wedge

## The Core Pain Point

### The "Trapped" Feeling

Many autistic people know they are awkward or missing something, but **cannot pinpoint what**.

Parents often feel trapped because:
- They cannot see what their child's school conversations are like
- Many kids cannot self-report what went wrong
- Kids often didn't recognize the social cue in the moment

**That's the wedge: Tizzy reduces the invisible friction.**

---

## Primary Market Segments

### 1. Kids Through Parents (Primary Buyer Segment)

**Buyer:** Parents of autistic children
**User:** The child
**Age Range:** 8-17 years old

#### Parent Motivations
- Want to help their child succeed socially
- Feel helpless about invisible school/social challenges
- Seeking concrete, measurable improvement tools
- Willing to pay for effective interventions

#### Challenges
- COPPA compliance for under-13
- Consent and privacy in school settings
- Need to avoid "fixing" narrative
- Must respect child's autonomy

#### Value Proposition
"Finally see what's actually happening in your child's day, and give them tools that actually help."

---

### 2. Big Transitions (Time-Bounded Wedge)

These are **excellent wedges** because they are:
- Time-bounded
- Emotionally intense
- Measurable
- High-motivation moments

#### Transition Types

| Transition | Age Range | Key Challenges | Tizzy Value |
|------------|-----------|----------------|-------------|
| Starting middle school | 11-13 | New social complexity, larger peer groups | Navigate new social hierarchy |
| Starting high school | 14-15 | Increased social sophistication expected | Reduce anxiety, build confidence |
| Moving schools | Any | Lost social capital, new norms | Accelerate social integration |
| Starting college | 18-22 | Independence, dorm life, unstructured time | Adjustment support, overload management |
| Starting new job | 18+ | Professional communication, office politics | Workplace communication coaching |
| Joining new social environments | Any | Club, team, community group | Community integration |

#### College Transition: The Strategic First Market

**Why college transitions are perfect:**

1. **Age 18+** - simpler consent, no COPPA
2. **High need** - overstimulation, social learning curve, adjustment-related overload
3. **Motivated users** - self-directed, want to succeed
4. **Measurable context** - semester timeframe, clear social goals
5. **Institutional partnerships** - disability services offices, counseling centers
6. **Parent willingness to pay** - even for adult children in transition

**Specific college pain points Tizzy addresses:**
- Roommate communication
- Class discussion participation
- Study group dynamics
- Social event navigation
- Dating and relationships
- RA/professor interactions
- Mental health check-ins

## Market Sizing

### Autism Prevalence

- **1 in 36 children** in the U.S. diagnosed with autism (CDC, 2023)
- Approximately **2.8%** of 8-year-olds
- **Source:** [CDC Autism Statistics](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/signs-symptoms/index.html)

### Addressable Market Segments

| Segment | Population (US) | Percentage Likely to Adopt | Addressable Market |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| Autistic children (ages 6-17) | ~850,000 | 15-20% (parents who can afford & are motivated) | 127,500 - 170,000 |
| College students with autism | ~50,000 new freshmen/year | 30-40% (high-transition need) | 15,000 - 20,000/year |
| Adults with autism diagnosis | ~2-3 million | 5-10% (self-improvement focused) | 100,000 - 300,000 |

### Initial Target: College Transition

**Year 1 Goal:** 500-1,000 college users
**Year 2 Goal:** 5,000-10,000 users across college + parent-driven
**Year 3 Goal:** 25,000-50,000 users with institutional partnerships

### Persona 1: Sarah, Parent of Middle Schooler

**Demographics:**
- Age 42, married, household income $85K
- Son Alex, age 12, diagnosed at age 7
- Suburban location, good school district

**Pain Points:**
- Alex is struggling more in middle school than elementary
- Teachers say he "doesn't read the room"
- Alex comes home upset but can't explain what happened

**Goals:**
- Help Alex make and keep friends
- Reduce meltdowns and social anxiety
- Understand what's actually happening at school
- Give Alex tools to succeed independently

**Buying Triggers:**
- School transition (elementary → middle school)
- Specific incident that revealed social gap
- Other autism parent recommendation
- Therapist suggestion

**Decision Criteria:**
- Privacy and safety
- Actually works (proof of outcomes)
- Not stigmatizing
- Reasonable cost ($50-150/month tolerance)

## Go-to-Market Sequence

### Wave 1: College Transition Program (Months 0-6)

**Why first:**
- 18+, simpler consent
- Highly motivated users
- Clear use case and timeframe
- Institutional partnership opportunities

**Channels:**
- Disability services offices
- Autism support organizations on campus
- College mental health centers
- Parent networks (paying for adult children)

**Success Metric:** 500-1,000 users proving 30-day value

---

### Wave 2: Speech-Language and Therapy Channel (Months 6-12)

**Why second:**
- B2B2C distribution leverage
- Professional endorsement builds trust
- Therapy multiplier narrative
- Clinician feedback improves product

**Channels:**
- SLP practices and clinics
- Autism therapy centers
- School-based therapists (pilot only)
- ABA providers (controversial, approach carefully)

**Success Metric:** 50-100 therapist partnerships, 2,000-5,000 users

---

### Wave 3: Parent-Driven Kids Adoption (Months 12-18)

**Why third:**
- Need proven trust posture first
- COPPA compliance must be bulletproof
- Word-of-mouth from Wave 1-2 users
- Privacy architecture validated

**Channels:**
- Autism parent Facebook groups
- Autism advocacy organizations
- Autism Speaks (if appropriate)
- School IEP recommendations

**Success Metric:** 10,000-20,000 users, mix of ages

---

### Wave 4: Schools (Months 18-24+)

**Why last:**
- FERPA compliance heavy lift
- Procurement cycles are slow
- Policies and legal review required
- Need proven outcomes data

**Channels:**
- Special education departments
- District-level pilots
- State education grants
- Title I funding

**Success Metric:** 5-10 school district pilots

## Competitive Positioning

### Current Landscape (Autism Tools)

**What exists:**
- Social skills apps (gamified, not real-world)
- Therapy/coaching (expensive, limited hours)
- Books and curricula (passive, not personalized)
- Video modeling (not adaptive)
- AAC devices (for different need)

**What doesn't exist:**
- Real-world, always-on conversation coaching
- Acoustic + social signal analysis
- Personalized, in-the-moment guidance
- Measurable outcomes tracking

**Tizzy's position:** The first real-world conversation coach for autism that combines always-on capture, voice-native AI, and privacy-first design.

## Summary: The Wedge Strategy

1. **Start with college transitions** - prove value with 18+ users
2. **Build therapist channel** - gain professional validation
3. **Expand to parent-driven** - scale with trust established
4. **Eventually schools** - institutional scale with proven compliance

**Core insight:** Autism is the wedge because:
- The pain is real and measurable
- The market is underserved
- The dataset moat is defensible
- The mission is meaningful
- The expansion path is clear (beyond autism later)

**The market is ready. The question is: can you build trust fast enough to capture it?**
