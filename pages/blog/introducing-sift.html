Introducing Sift

We spent last year running support agents for other companies, and the hardest part was never building them.

It was finding out, days later, that one had been telling customers something false since Tuesday. No alert fired. Error rates were flat. Latency was fine. A customer emailed, then another, and someone opened a run by hand and saw it.

Every team we talked to had the same story and the same workaround: a person reading a sample of runs every morning. It catches some things. It costs a senior engineer an hour a day. It misses anything rare.

Sift is the tool we wanted then.

What it does today

Sift reads every production run your agents make and checks the final answer against what actually happened in the run. When a claim has no support, when an action was reported but never completed, or when the agent loops on a tool, Sift marks the step and tells you why.

  • Replay. Every run, step by step, with the inputs and outputs of each tool call.
  • Grading. Each run checked against your instructions and against its own tool results.
  • Grouping. Failures that share a cause collapse into one issue, with a count and a first-seen date.
  • Alerts. A message in your team channel when a new issue appears or an old one comes back.

Setup is two environment variables and one line around your agent's entry point. The quickstart walks through it.

Who it is for

Teams with an agent already in front of customers, usually support, onboarding, scheduling or internal operations, who have been surprised by it at least once. If your agent is still in a notebook, you do not need us yet.

What comes next

Sift is in public beta from today. We are a small team, we answer every email ourselves, and we would like to hear about the failure that made you look for something like this.
