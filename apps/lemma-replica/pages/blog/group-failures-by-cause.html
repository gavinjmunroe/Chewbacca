Group failures by cause, not by trace

July 21, 2026
Ines Okafor Applied ML, Sift
6 min read
  1. Two hundred flags, four problems
  2. Why the text misleads
  3. The failure signature
  4. What changed for teams

A busy agent that fails one run in fifty produces a queue nobody can read.

The first version of Sift flagged runs one at a time. Teams loved it for a week and then stopped opening it, because a morning with two hundred flagged runs is the same as a morning with none: you cannot act on either.

When we sat with those queues by hand, the two hundred were almost always four or five problems, each repeated many times with different customers and different wording.

Why the text misleads

The obvious approach is to cluster runs by the text of the final answer. It fails in both directions. Two runs that both say "I've updated your address" can fail for unrelated reasons, one because the tool timed out and one because it wrote to the wrong account. And two runs with nothing in common on the surface can share a cause, because the same stale value fed both.

The failure signature

So we group on what went wrong rather than what was said. For each flagged run Sift records a short signature:

  • the kind of failure: unsupported claim, unfinished action, repeated call, broken instruction
  • the tool or instruction involved
  • the step where the run first diverged from what it reported

Runs with matching signatures land in one issue. The issue keeps every run, so you can still open any one of them, but you read the cause once. 

issue  unfinished action / create_credit / status "queued" read as done
runs   37 in the last 7 days, first seen Jul 9

What changed for teams

The queue became short enough to finish. A team that had stopped looking now closes issues instead of skimming runs, and when a fixed issue comes back, it comes back as the same issue with its history attached, not as a fresh pile.
