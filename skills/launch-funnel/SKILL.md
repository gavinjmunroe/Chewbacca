# launch-funnel

A landing page is the last link in a chain: who it's for, what to say to
them, the ad that brings them, the page that collects their email, and the
page that takes their deposit. This skill writes the chain in that order,
following ACAD 185 (Prof. Chris Swain, USC Iovine and Young Academy, Fall
2026), whose numbers come from the paid test in [[venture-validation]].

Write in order. A hero headline written before the persona is a guess about
who's reading it.

## 1. The persona

Build it from evidence (Mom Test calls, survey answers), never invented. Group
what you observed into activities, motivations, aptitudes and skills, then
name the archetype and give it a face. One persona is enough. With more than
one, they can't overlap, and the primary is the one no other persona's design
would satisfy.

The course questionnaire has 11 parts: demographics; where to find them (top
5 sites, newsletters and podcasts, communities, top 5 influencers); goals and
dream outcome; pain points, the trigger event and objections; how they buy and
research (name the exact site or magazine); brand perceptions and the
competitors they'd consider; psychographics and a day in the life; the
anti-persona (who this is NOT); who they turn to for approval; when they're
most likely to act; and logic vs emotion, the emotional triggers, the logical
questions, the tone. Always write the anti-persona.

## 2. Messaging and direction

"Up to 80% of conversion performance" is how well the message lands, so this
gets written before any page. The framework:

- One-sentence value proposition (the hook).
- Three or four pillars of value that support it.
- Proof points for each pillar: data, testimonials, features.
- The "so what": say why it matters, not what it is.

The Messaging and Direction Summary (MDS) carries it: what the product is;
features split into difference and parity; three primary values, each as
feature, benefits and feelings; secondary values; problems; benefits; Meta
audiences and influencers; use cases and the aha moment; messaging ideas and
tone; product list, how to use, pricing (retail, lowest offer, COGS,
break-even ROAS, target CPA); the judgments and questions people will have;
and the non-negotiables for the creative.

Brand check (Keller's CBBE pyramid, as the course teaches it): salience (what
category, who competes), performance (points of parity and difference, price
tier), imagery, judgments (write three fake reviews), feelings, resonance.

## 3. Competitor teardown

Per competitor, seven answers: price; key messaging quoted from their site;
landing page screenshots; ad screenshots (Meta Ad Library); points of
difference; what reviews praise; what reviews complain about. Quote real
reviews with where they came from. Capture pages headless, never in the
user's visible browser.

To reverse engineer a landing page, go section by section: the section title,
a screenshot, the exact text, an image description, and the section's purpose
in landing page terms (never in product terms), then write the same section
for your own product next to it.

## 4. The ad

Primary text: "This [category] has everything you need," then the feature
lines, then the offer. An image or video that shows the product in use.
Headline: the offer ("Get huge prelaunch discounts"). The ad has to match the
page it lands on, or L-CR drops.

## 5. The landing page

- Section 1, hero. Headline is the unique selling proposition in 7 to 15
  words: "The world's [superlative] [category] to do [value 1] and
  [value 2]." Subtext: "[Product] is a [keyword] [category] with a [key
  feature using a number]," then the values in reverse order as full
  sentences. Or the list form: headline "The world's [superlative] [value 1],
  [value 2] [category]," then at least 3 key features.
- Section 2, sign-up form. "We're launching soon. Get an exclusive discount
  by signing up now!" Button: "Reserve my launch invite."
- Sections 3 to 5, headlines of 7 to 9 words, subtext either a list or a
  paragraph under two sentences, and never both together. Section 3 shows how you're
  similar to what they know (familiarity). Section 4 breaks expectations with
  the most differentiating feature. Section 5 shows the feature that best
  represents the mission. Section types: features, benefits, pain points,
  use cases, how to use, testimonials, founder story.
- 90% of the traffic is on a phone. Write and check every section at phone
  width.

## 6. The reservation page

"Save X% when you reserve [product] today!" A $1 deposit locks the discount
off the regular price, and the page says the final total. Show the five
numbers: deposit, discounted price, regular price, the discount in dollars,
the discount percent, and check the math before shipping it.

## 7. Visual storytelling on the page

From Bruce Block's visual structure, as the course applies it: contrast
raises intensity and grabs attention, affinity lowers it and feels calm. Use
contrast where the eye should go (the CTA, the headline) and affinity
everywhere else. The levers are space, line, shape, tone, color, movement and
rhythm. Diagonal lines and vanishing points pull the eye; a complementary hue
makes a button stand out (a red button on a green page), analogous hues keep
the reader flowing. Brightness decides what's seen first. Movement is seen
before anything else. Pick color by the feeling it carries: blue for trust,
green for growth or health, orange for friendly, black for premium.

## Never

- Never write page copy before the persona and the value proposition exist.
- Never claim a number, review or customer the product doesn't have. Mark
  untested prices and features as a best guess.
- Never describe a section's purpose in product terms during a teardown.
- Never ship the reservation math without recomputing it.
- Never put body text and a bullet list in the same section.
