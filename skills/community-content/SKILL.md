# community-content

Social content is how a product gets free traffic and test data before it has
an ad budget. This is the method from ACAD 185's two Social Artifact lectures
(Prof. Chris Swain, USC Iovine and Young Academy, Fall 2026), plus Seth
Godin's "The Tribes We Lead," which the course assigns alongside it.

## Pick the account type and the niche first

Three kinds of account work. Pick one before writing a single hook:

- Brand: content promotes the brand directly (Final Boss Sour).
- Community: content serves the community around the product, and is not
  about the product (a makeup creator who built an audience for years before
  launching her own line).
- Founder image: content follows the founder's journey, failures and
  behind-the-scenes included (Minted New York).

A niche is the intersection of three concepts, written about the brand and
never the product (the deck's example sits at hydration, edgy content and
90s rock culture). Bold one as the anchor and study what already works in that anchor.
Weird niches work as long as they're authentic.

Godin's test for the community: who are you upsetting, who are you
connecting, who are you leading. You don't invent the want, you lead people
who already have it. "The Beatles did not invent teenagers."

## The 80/20 rule

80% of posts serve the community. 20% are about the product. A feed that's
all product is an ad account, and the algorithm treats it like one.

## The pipeline

Keep a CMS (a sheet or Notion board) with these columns, in order: Hook/Ideas,
Greenlit Concepts, Scripts Written, Pre-Production, Production, Editing,
Published. Every idea carries its owner's name.

1. Research into a content bank, tagged by Trends, Sounds, Styles, Hooks and
   Content/Value. Use three accounts: your own (train it on what you like),
   the community account (train it on what the community likes), and a real
   member's last 10 liked videos. Also the TikTok Creative Center, Creator
   Search Insights, top comments, blue comments (TikTok's own search links),
   Google Keyword Planner and Reddit.
2. Ideate by synthesizing (combine a community with a format, like
   sustainability plus the quiz trend, or restructure a format, like moving
   the punchline to the front so the video loops) or by simulating (borrow a
   proven hook or structure and change the body). Simulate when nothing is
   working.
3. Greenlight the best ideas and produce them. Use AI for research, ideation and first drafts, but
   the finished piece has to show judgment: a generic AI video is the floor,
   not the bar.
4. When you post, pick the time from research on this community. Keyword-rich captions
   beat hashtags. Seed early engagement: friends comment, share to stories,
   write the first comment yourself. "Just ship it and learn."
5. Review the numbers every day and change the next post based on them.

## What the algorithm rewards

TikTok shows a video to a small test group, then pushes it in larger waves
while engagement holds. The numbers the course gives:

- Post about once a day. Consistency is rewarded.
- A strong hook keeps the 2 second bounce low. Hook types: a visual surprise,
  a claim, a question, on-screen text that teases the value, a pattern
  interrupt, a promise of value.
- Average watch time above 21 seconds.
- Like to view ratio above 1 in 10 (10%).
- Comments come from a call to action, a tease, controversy or real value.
- Follower growth per month: 8 to 10% for an emerging account, 5 to 8% mid
  tier, 2 to 4% large. Per video, 0.5 to 1% of viewers following is solid, 1
  to 2% very strong, above 2% exceptional.
- Follow rate is niche strength times series strength: people follow a
  series they want more of in a niche they care about.
- Search traffic counts: accounts whose views come from Google and TikTok
  search get rewarded.

## Reading a flop

Check, in order: was the bounce at 2 seconds high (the hook), was watch time
under 21 seconds (the body or the length), was like to view under 10% (the
value), did nobody follow (no series, or the niche is unclear). Fix the first
one that failed. Caleb's Kyber A14 video got 1 view: posted once, no seeded
engagement, on a day-old account, which is the posting step, not the idea.

## Never

- Never write product-first content for a community account.
- Never ship a hook that needs the second sentence to make sense.
- Never post and walk away: seed the first engagement in the first hour.
- Never judge a format from one post on a new account.
