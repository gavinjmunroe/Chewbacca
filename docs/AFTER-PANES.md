# After panes

For Caleb, Gavin, Jake and Semyon. Written 2026-10-05, after Caleb said: "The opal OS is a billion times prettier and it is rlly a bad ux, just windows, we need to build what comes next after a bunch of panes."

This doc covers why panes are the old idea, the three directions we drew up, how three judges scored them, the design we recommend, a day in it, what it removes, the risks, a one-week prototype on the current renderer and graph, and the test that would show it failed. Sources are linked inline. The full list at the end separates what was actually read from what was seen only as a snippet or not opened at all.

## Why panes are the old idea

Opal's desktop puts ten frosted panels up at once. The same transcript appears in two of them, nothing says which one matters now, and one green Go pill means both "add to calendar" and "message Nadia" (teardown in [kyber-surfaces SKILL.md](../.claude/skills/kyber-surfaces/SKILL.md), under "The bar to clear"). Kyber today runs on the same idea. A surface is a panel on the glass, several open at once is described as the normal case in [hud/CLAUDE.md](../hud/CLAUDE.md), and a right Rail launches more of them.

Windows were a fix for a small screen. Mark Weiser wrote in 1991 that windows were invented "as a way of fitting several different activities onto the small space of a computer screen at the same time," and asked "who would ever use a desk whose surface area is only 9" by 11"?" He called pads "an antidote to windows" ([Weiser, The Computer for the 21st Century](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~corps/phaseii/Weiser-Computer21stCentury-SciAm.pdf)). Jason Yuan made the same point about the glass itself: strip away "all the Aero and Paper and Frosted Glass" and what's left is "a skeleton of XEROX PARC's 1973 invention" ([The desktop metaphor must die](https://web.archive.org/web/20220123181213/https://uxdesign.cc/the-desktop-metaphor-must-die-676fbb34afdb)). Opal is prettier chrome on the same model.

People attend to one thing at a time. Jef Raskin built The Humane Interface around a single locus of attention ([summary on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Humane_Interface); we could not open the book, so treat his wording as paraphrase). Apple's own spatial guidance agrees: "Ideally, keep your app's interface in a single window. Multiple windows can quickly become a lot for people to manage" ([WWDC23, Principles of spatial design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10072/)). Opal breaks three Apple rules at once. It puts glass on the content layer, which the HIG says not to do ([Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). It opens many windows by default ([Windows](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows)). And it runs a live waveform at the edge of the screen, where motion pulls the eye on its own ([Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)).

Panes aren't always wrong. When a comparison involves more than about three things, multiple windows beat zooming, and zoom users made significantly more errors (Plumlee and Ware, as summarized in [Bederson 2011](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~bederson/images/pubs_pdfs/2011_-_BIT_-_Promise_of_ZUIs.pdf); we did not read the original papers). So panes are right for comparing and wrong as the default arrangement. The Opal mistake is showing ten things before anyone has asked a question.

Every past attempt to replace the desktop died the same way. The Canon Cat was pulled within about six months ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Cat)). Archy stalled after Raskin died and never shipped its zoom ([Wikipedia](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archy_(software)>)). Enso was discontinued and open-sourced ([dundalek](https://dundalek.com/entropic/enso-launcher/)). Mercury OS and Desktop Neo were never built ([Mercury](https://web.archive.org/web/20201101000953/https://uxdesign.cc/introducing-mercury-os-f4de45a04289), [Neo](https://desktopneo.com/)). The zoomable desktop "never materialised," in the words of one of the people who built it ([Bederson 2011](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~bederson/images/pubs_pdfs/2011_-_BIT_-_Promise_of_ZUIs.pdf)). Each one asked strangers to learn a whole new world before any single task got easier. What survived was bolted onto existing systems: incremental search, autosave, the command palette, tiling.

The recent attempts failed in two more ways. The Humane AI Pin and Rabbit R1 removed the screen, and every task got slower than the phone in your pocket ([Brownlee on the Pin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TitZV6k8zfA), [on the R1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ddTV12hErTc); Humane sold to HP for $116M per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/18/humanes-ai-pin-is-dead-as-hp-buys-startups-assets-for-116m)). Generated UI looks great and takes 30 to 90 seconds or more to appear ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-dynamic-view-3619662/)), while flow breaks at one second. And Arc showed what new concepts cost: only 5.52% of daily users used more than one Space ([Josh Miller's letter](https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/letter-to-arc-members-2025)).

That gives us four constraints. Show one thing at a time. Sit on top of macOS and keep every exit open. Draw from the local graph with no model in the way. Spend novelty only where it pays off in the first minute.

## The three directions

**Focus Walk.** The glass holds one graph node at a time, titled by its own content. Under it sits a rim of its typed edges, written as words with counts. Taking an edge slides the current node left into a receded trail and brings the target in from the right, keyed by osgraph node id. Three quiet slots at one edge (just happened, now, next) grow in place into the focus. It is the best model of navigation of the three. It is also a browser for the graph rather than a day: the center starts empty, so a stranger has to pull everything.

**The Docket.** One card in the center holds the single decision that needs you now, with one to three verbs taken from that node's type, one of them always "Not now." Behind it is a stable queue built from the existing `_attention()` tiers in [osgraph_walks.py](../bin/lib/osgraph_walks.py) (BLOCKED, then DUE, then FYI). The head of the queue is frozen, so new arrivals never reshuffle what you can see. Glanceable slots show what just happened, what's happening now and what's next. Everything else is a held key away.

**Summoned Answer.** The glass is empty apart from a three-slot strip of words by the hyper bar. Holding Option-Space or the talk key and naming something puts one view in the center. The view is built on one node and shows only that node's verbs. When the verb reads back, or on Esc, the view shrinks into the strip token it grew from. Pinning saves a named intention that is recomputed each time it's summoned, so it never becomes a standing panel. It is the purest pane-killer, and it leaves a stranger staring at a blank glass wondering what to ask.

## The judges

Three judges scored each direction from 1 to 10 on four things: a stranger's first ten minutes, luxury without tackiness, how buildable it is on the current renderer, and how fully it kills panes. They also counted presses for five real jobs (reply to Gavin, the WRIT 150 essay, a Claude session waiting on a permission, a Zeutara campaign reply, shipping a fix to main) against about 24 to 25 presses in today's apps.

| Direction       | Stranger | Luxury  | Buildable | Kills panes | Total | Presses, five jobs |
| --------------- | -------- | ------- | --------- | ----------- | ----- | ------------------ |
| Focus Walk      | 6, 5, 5  | 8, 8, 8 | 6, 4, 4   | 8, 9, 8     | 79    | about 15           |
| The Docket      | 7, 7, 6  | 7, 7, 6 | 8, 7, 7   | 8, 8, 6     | 84    | about 8 to 12      |
| Summoned Answer | 5, 5, 6  | 7, 7, 7 | 7, 7, 8   | 9, 9, 9     | 86    | about 14           |

**The Docket won, two judges to one.** Summoned Answer has the higher raw total because it scores 9 on killing panes from every judge. The two majority judges picked the Docket anyway. Theirs was the only direction a stranger understands in the first minute: a card asking a plain question, three labeled verbs that each state their cost, and "Not now." It had the lowest press count because the decision comes to you, and its prototype reuses `_attention()`, `rank()`, the `to` chip and the rule-10 morph.

The third judge picked Summoned Answer, and that judge's objection is the strongest one against the Docket. A card permanently in the center is wrong for an overlay that sits over VS Code all day. That judge also counted about six bare surfaces in the Docket's rim, strip, trail and card. The recommendation below takes the Docket's core and adopts that objection.

All three judges agreed on four facts. Grow-in-place from a corner slot to the center is a transform across two surfaces, and the renderer can't do it yet: `matchedGeometryEffect` exists only in `hud/Sources/KyberKit/Launcher.swift`. The overlay never takes key, so every typed reply goes through the hyper bar's `to` chip ([hud/CLAUDE.md](../hud/CLAUDE.md), line 147). The permission job saves no presses in any direction. The Zeutara job doesn't work in any direction until something writes MailItem nodes from [replies.py](../../work/zeutara-gtme/scripts/replies.py).

## The recommended design

It's the Docket's queue and card, inside Summoned Answer's frame, with Focus Walk's rim and trail.

**Resting state.** The center is empty. One strip of three word slots sits by the hyper bar: JUST HAPPENED, NOW, NEXT. A slot shows at most two tokens, and further tokens rotate in, because two visible is the number Apple ships in [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities). A token is a name or word plus a count or time. It never shows a message body, because the glass is public. Nothing moves while idle except a digit rolling when a value changes. Nothing takes the center on its own. This is the third judge's point, and it also follows Mercury's rule that "information will not be pushed to the user unless they intentionally ask for it."

**The card.** One press on the strip, or saying "what needs me," puts the head of the queue in the center. The card always sits on one graph node and is titled by that node's content ("Gavin: can u merge the router fix tonight"), never by an app name. Its first line answers the question a stranger would bring ("Gavin Munroe is waiting · 2 h · iMessage"), following Bret Victor's question "What questions will the viewer ask?" ([Magic Ink](https://worrydream.com/MagicInk/)). At most one content surface is open at a time, and a test enforces that.

**Verbs.** Each card has one to three verbs, one of them accented, with "Not now" always present. They come from a single table keyed by osgraph node type, so a verb is defined once and works wherever that node appears. That is Raskin's "0 applications, 1 system, N user documents, hundreds of commands" applied to our graph. Under each verb, a muted line says what it touches and whether it can be undone.

**Two classes of action.** Graph-only verbs (snooze, done, close, block time) happen at once, with no confirm dialog, and append their inverse to an undo ledger that survives restarts. Outbound verbs (iMessage send, push, anything under Jonah's name) are drafted acts: the target is shown, Go fires on release, and the result is read back into the card in the same frame. In the [Operator system card](https://cdn.openai.com/operator_system_card.pdf), 5 of 13 errors in 100 tasks were irreversible, and confirmations cut that risk by about 90%.

**The queue.** The order is `_attention()` passed through `rank()` with `PER_SOURCE=3`, so one source can't flood it. The visible head is frozen and saved to a state file, and a new item lands inside its tier behind what you can already see. Static beat adaptive in [Findlater and McGrenere](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/th/2004/Findlater2004/Findlater2004.pdf), and Stage Manager lost trust by moving things without asking ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macos-ventura-the-macstories-review/2/)). A strip under the card shows the next two items and a count, nothing more.

**Not now.** Not now takes a time ("after class," "tonight," "tomorrow 9"), writes `snoozed_until` on the node, and puts the item in the NEXT slot with its return time. On the third snooze of the same node, the card offers "Close it" or "Hand to someone" instead. A batch verb, "clear 6 FYI," clears the FYI tier and can be undone as one step, so one-at-a-time never turns into a chore.

**Edge rim and trail.** Under the card is a row of at most five typed edges with counts ("Gavin Munroe · Chewbacca · 1 task from this thread"), in a fixed slot order per node type and drawn as Buttons. Taking one re-addresses the same card to that node. The last three nodes walked stay as a still trail at the card's left edge, and Esc walks back along it. Because the trail states the path without motion, a missed animation still leaves the path readable, which is [Bederson's](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~bederson/images/pubs_pdfs/2011_-_BIT_-_Promise_of_ZUIs.pdf) warning about zoom transitions. It also covers the [knowledge-graph finding](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.01311v4) that users preferred a table to a node-link view. We never draw the graph itself.

**Honesty on every guess.** Any card built on an inferred edge shows "(guess)," the source message, and a "wrong" verb that gets counted. AWAITS_REPLY_FROM is right on 8 of 12 held-out threads ([KYBER-SURFACES.md](KYBER-SURFACES.md)). Until held-out precision clears about 90%, guessed items stay in FYI and never reach the head. Sources the daemon can't read (Jonah's mailboxes, Slack, WhatsApp) show as a named "not connected" edge on the Project, so a hole never reads as "nothing pending."

**Intentions.** Saying "WRIT 150 until 11" or "Zeutara for 20 min" scopes the queue to that Space's subgraph and silences the strip. "Pin this as Zeutara" saves the walk as a named token through `kyber-genui --pin`. It is recomputed whenever it's summoned and is never a live panel. Only `urgency=critical` breaks through.

**Compare is the only pane.** "Compare" on two to four nodes of one type opens them side by side on a shared axis, and they fold back when he's done. A Diff beside a ship card counts as a comparison.

**Material.** Content sits on a thick, nearly opaque, backlit material. Glass is used only on the strip and the bar. With a card open, the desktop behind it dims by about 35%, the HIG's number for a dimming layer. The type family is one family with strong size contrast, and radii nest concentrically with even margins. Walks run on bounce-0 springs at about 300 to 500 ms; 500 ms animations cut reading errors 54% in Klein and Bederson's study. Under Reduce Motion they become crossfades. The one novel moment is the strip assembling for the first time on a fresh Mac. Rauno Freiberg's rule is 90% familiar, 10% novel, spent on once-only moments ([Novelty](https://rauno.me/craft/novelty)). Luxury here comes from restraint and fit, not from more blur.

**First frame on an empty graph.** On a fresh Mac with no grants, the strip reads "Type a name, a class, or what's due," with each source that's off named, for example "Messages not connected." A stranger gets search, which they already know, before anything new.

## Caleb's day in it

**Gavin texts.** chat.db ingest writes a Thread awaiting Caleb, which is tier BLOCKED. Nothing pops up. JUST HAPPENED changes to "Gavin · 1." He presses it and the card opens on the thread, titled with Gavin's last message. He presses Reply, the hyper bar becomes "To Gavin," he types, and the send button reads "Send on iMessage to Gavin Munroe · can't be undone." The handle is re-read from chat.db at the press and the message is read back. The card shows "Sent 9:41, read back," then dissolves. Messages.app never opened. Group threads walk to the Person instead, because group replies are not sent from the glass. That's about three presses plus typing, the same as Messages. The gain is that he never had to go looking.

**The WRIT 150 essay.** NEXT shows "WRIT 150 · Sun." Before the card claims anything, the daemon runs `coursework due --json` and `coursework policy writ150 ai`. The card is titled with the assignment's name. The first line is the due date with its ledger source, and the AI policy is shown word for word. The verbs are Block 90 min (a calendar event he can undo), Open draft (the real file in its real editor), and Not now. Kyber never drafts the essay and never submits it. Its job ends at deciding when he writes. One or two presses instead of about eight.

**A Claude session waiting on a permission.** NOW shows "lemma · waiting." The card shows the exact prompt from the hook board, the project, the changed files, and "Approve it in the session. Kyber never grants permissions." The verbs are Bring it forward and Open diff. This saves no presses over VS Code, and we shouldn't sell it as a win. The gain is that the prompt doesn't sit unseen for an hour.

**A Zeutara reply.** This job needs a new ingester first: run replies.py, read the reply text rather than Clay's "Interested" label, and write MailItem nodes into the amber Space. The 10-05 "Interested" reply was a Hustle Fund form redirect, and only the text shows that. The card leads with that text. The sender shows as the raw address marked "unverified sender," because nothing is fused on a name. The verbs are Draft for Jonah, which puts a draft beside the original (sending stays in Clay, by hand, under Jonah's name), Canned and close, and Not now.

**Shipping a fix.** A repo with a finished session and uncommitted work becomes a DUE card: "chewbacca · main · 3 files changed · tests green." Open diff puts the Diff beside the card, which is the comparison case. Ship routes "/ship" into that repo's session through `kyber-sessions send`, and the card names the files and branch it will touch. Kyber never runs git itself. CI's result comes back later as a JUST HAPPENED token, and a red result enters the queue as BLOCKED, titled with the failing check.

## What it kills

Several panels open as the normal case. Left, middle and right column placement. Folding the least-recent panel into a corner. The right Rail of unlabeled icon wells. Opal's bottom bar (Interface, Ask, Needs you, Customize). The nine-row needs-you list, which becomes the queue feeding one card. Toasts and banners. Confirm dialogs on reversible graph actions. Glass on content, rows with no verb, and one Go pill that means different things on different rows. Free-form generated panels: `kyber-genui` may choose which walk and which fixed card type to show, never write the card.

It also kills the app trips made only to check something: Messages to see who's owed, Mail and Clay for replies, Calendar and Brightspace to see what's due, Terminal to see if a session is stuck. Those apps stay for real work, one Open away.

The graph, the walks, Go and its hard gates, the `to` chip, the hyper bar, and the Diff, File and Transcript components all stay as they are.

## Risks

**A wrong head card is expensive.** At 8 of 12, a wrong "waiting on you" at the head costs his whole attention, where the same mistake at row seven of a list costs a glance. Mitigations: the 90% bar for guesses, the "(guess)" label with its source, and a counted "wrong" verb.

**Loss of peripheral awareness.** People want to see the running transfer or the Slack channel ([HN on Desktop Neo](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20156168)). If the strip is too thin, he'll reopen the apps. If testers pin five intentions, panes are back under another name.

**Not now becomes a graveyard.** The three-snooze cap is the guard.

**Typing through the bar feels like a mode.** If the hop from card to hyper bar is slow, replies drift back to Messages.app, so the cold test below includes a reply.

**Empty graph on a stranger's Mac.** No chat.db or Calendar grants means a blank strip. The first frame has to read as familiar search and name what's off. The onboarding grants Gavin is working on come first.

**Model latency creeps into the frame.** If drafting or classification moves into the render path, the card misses one second and starts to feel like the Pin. A test times the card draw with no model process running.

**Screen share.** The card shows message snippets in the center. It needs a one-press blur, on by default while screen sharing is detected.

**Motion done badly.** Cross-surface transforms and shared-axis slides are new Swift, and that is where the 10-04 "chunky tab loading" bug lived. The prototype ships in-place morphs of one container and defers the rest.

**Judged on screenshots.** One dimmed card loses a still-frame comparison to ten Opal panels. It has to be judged from a recording. "The interactive demo... is really worth a million static designs" ([WWDC18, Designing Fluid Interfaces](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)).

## One-week prototype

This uses the current HUD wire format and component vocabulary, plus one Swift prop.

**Day 1, the queue and the card.** Add `bin/lib/surfaces/docket.py`, registered as kind `docket` in `bin/kyber-surfaces`. The queue is `_attention(g, now)` passed through `rank()`, then a frozen head saved to `~/.bob/docket-state.json`, with items whose `props.snoozed_until` is later than now filtered out. The card is `@ docket at=center w=520`: a Screen titled with the node's content, a question line, a provenance line (source, confidence, "(guess)"), and a row of up to three Buttons with one `variant=primary`. Resolving re-addresses the same `@ docket` with the same component ids, so the existing rule-10 morph handles the transition with no new Swift. The trail is a three-row List with `action=ks-pivot`.

**Day 2, verbs and the Zeutara ingester.** Add `bin/lib/surfaces/verbs.py`, a table from node type to at most three verbs, each with label, touches and undoable. A 1:1 Thread gets Reply (through the `to docket` chip and the existing send path with read-back), Not now, and Person. A MailItem gets Draft, Close and Not now. An Assignment gets Block 90 min, Open and Not now. An agent Task gets Bring forward, Open diff and Not now. Presses arrive as `e <action> <component> surface=docket` under a `ks-docket-*` prefix. Then add the ingester in `osgraph_ingest.py` that runs replies.py and writes MailItem nodes with `network=clay`, unverified senders, and confidence below 1 on any inferred AWAITS_REPLY_FROM.

**Day 3, the strip, Not now and undo.** `@ locus` sits by the pill, `chrome=bare urgency=ambient`, with three slots of two tokens each, each value a bound Metric so an update is one `d` line and only the digit rolls. Pressing a token opens the card on that node. Stop drawing the Rail. Not now writes `snoozed_until` through Graph. `~/.bob/docket-undo.jsonl` stores the verb, node, inverse and time for snooze, done, close and block, and JUST HAPPENED carries "Undo <verb>" for 30 minutes. Graph writes carry `resolved_by=docket`, so `kyber-surfaces forget` still clears everything.

**Day 4, the rim, intentions and the first frame.** Add `focus_edges(g, node_id)` to [osgraph_walks.py](../bin/lib/osgraph_walks.py), returning at most five edges in a fixed per-type order, generic over EDGE_TYPES in both directions. Draw them as Buttons. Map "school space" and "zeutara mode", which [surface_intent.py](../bin/lib/surface_intent.py) already parses, to `_attention(scope=...)`, and silence the strip while a scope is active. Add the empty-graph first frame, with each source that's off named.

**Day 5, Swift and the gates.** Add a `focus=dim` prop on `@` in `hud/Sources/Kyber`. It draws a 35% scrim behind that surface and draws content on the thick material, fades in on the existing spring, and becomes a plain fade under Reduce Motion. Add a SurfaceMotionTests case asserting the scrim never animates while idle. In `tests/test_osgraph.py` and `tests/surfaces_fixture.py`, add a fixture day with a 1:1 text, WRIT 150 due within 72 hours, an agent waiting on permission, a canned Zeutara reply and a dirty repo. Assert these: at most one ks content surface is open, the head order is unchanged after a new FYI arrives, every card has a noun and one to three verbs with exactly one primary, Not now followed by Undo restores the queue exactly, a send button names the network and "can't be undone," a guess below the precision bar never reaches the head, and the card draws with no model process running.

Then record the five jobs with Cap from the running build, not mockups.

## The test that would show it failed

The goal is that he stops opening the apps, so app usage decides it, not passing tests and not how the recording looks.

Take three days of app activations and foreground hours from knowledgeC.db before the switch for Messages, Mail, Calendar, Terminal and VS Code-for-checking, plus Chrome on Brightspace and Clay. Then take the same for the prototype week. The existing baseline in [OS-COVERAGE.md](OS-COVERAGE.md) is Messages at 7.1 hours over 14 days. Count from the logs, never from impressions.

It failed if Messages hours and activations don't drop by at least a third against that baseline during the week. That threshold is a guess, never measured, and the week's data should set the real one. It also failed if he reopens the old panel surfaces, pins more than three intentions, or presses "wrong" on more than one head card in five.

Separately, after `kyber-surfaces forget`, two people who aren't Caleb get one cold minute on his Mac. If either can't reach a message from a named person and reply to it without being told how, the first frame failed, whatever the knowledgeC numbers say.
