# The fridge: what to build next

Written 2026-10-07 after Caleb's review of the draft: "Basic af each element
has the same ux, nothing diff/unexpected & cool abt this." Every item did one
thing, swing on hover and open a side panel on click. The fix is to give each
object the interaction that object really has in a kitchen, and to tie that
interaction to what Amber remembers about the person behind it.

## What Caleb called terrible on the draft, 2026-10-07

"Most of this site looks terrible", with a screenshot of the join card. What
that card gets wrong, and the pattern behind it:

- The form lines and the paper's ruling are two separate grids, so the inputs
  sit between rules and the card shows double lines. One set of rules, and
  the inputs sit on them.
- The title is set in Bradley Hand, the font look already rejected on the
  notes. Anything handwritten on the page gets drawn with the pen engine, or
  it isn't handwritten at all.
- The body copy fell back to the old site's serif, so the page has three
  voices: a fake hand, an editorial serif and system UI. Pick one UI face and
  stop there.
- The fat amber gradient button and the pill-shaped Close are stock web UI
  laid on top of a physical card. On an object, the action should be a
  physical act: pinning the card, a magnet that clicks.
- Every panel is a web overlay. The objects stay objects when opened: the card
  you fill in is the card that gets pinned, and nothing slides in from the
  side.
- Before showing anything again, screenshot every state at 1440 and 390 and
  run both standards in the handwriting skill against each one. Caleb found
  this in one look.

## Each object proves a different thing Amber does

Caleb, same night: "Wtf is the point of diff ux if they're all emphasizing the
same thing". Eight interactions that all land on "Amber remembers a fact about
someone" are one idea in eight costumes. Each object has to demonstrate a
different capability, and its interaction has to be that capability working.
Every capability below exists in an Amber repo today; check the current code
before claiming it on the page.

| Object | What it proves | The interaction is the feature | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sagar's letter | Why Amber exists | It unfolds along its fold and reads as his note | Sagar's July 2026 post |
| Polaroid of five friends | Groups, not just people | Opening it runs a "When is everyone free?" group card; five rows fill in, one per friend, each answered by that friend's own Amber | amber-circles group cards (Free time, Vote, Who knows) |
| Ana's postcard | Voice is the input | Her voice memo plays, and you watch the sentences become kept facts on her card | amber-ios voice notes |
| Priya and Dev's save the date | The network | It opens to the path between them with you in the middle, the introduction you made | PeopleRank and the network graph on /network |
| Dad's appointment card | Health, the half every pitch drops | The blood pressure log he keeps forgetting, plotted, and a reminder to bring it | the health dimensions in amber-id and amber-ios |
| Marcus's race bib | Shared goals | Two people's training side by side, and a nudge when one falls behind | the six dimensions on the contact card |
| Sticky note, "call Lola" | Drift | Days since you last talked, counting, and the call button | ranked rows that state their reason on /network |
| Maya's drawing | What people love | Her interests now (volcanoes), her birthday, and a gift idea that fits | contact card memory |
| The amber magnets | You own what is kept | Holding one shows what is inside and who can see it | privacy tiers in amber-id |

Each row's interaction should look and feel unlike the others, because each
one is showing a different machine. Two objects showing the same capability
means one of them goes.

## The room reacts

- Hovering reacts to the material: the sticky note lifts at its free edge, the
  letter flutters from the top, the polaroid barely moves, the bib swings on
  its pins.
- Hovering also has sound per material: a card's snap, a sticky note's soft
  peel, the compressor hum when the fridge opens, a kettle in the morning
  light.
- Turn the clock hands and the day time-lapses through the three renders,
  with the light sweeping across the door.
- The tree in the window follows the real season, with LA jacarandas in
  purple in May.
- At night the cursor is a flashlight, and the paper only reads where you
  point it.
- Layered renders (wall, fridge, paper) give mouse parallax on desktop and
  gyro tilt on a phone, where the paper swings when you tilt.
- The phone on the counter buzzes now and then with an Amber text, like the
  real iMessage agent, and tapping it opens the thread.
- The copy follows the time of day: "Who are you seeing today?" in the
  morning, "Who did you think about today?" at night.

## Joining is the peak

- Write your name with your finger or mouse instead of typing it. The strokes
  are rendered by the pen engine on real paper, and the card gets pinned with
  an amber magnet that drops in with a real contact shadow.
- A shared fridge: the next visitor sees a few real cards left by earlier
  people, moderated, first names only.
- "Put a friend on your fridge": your friend's card appears in pencil, and
  when they join it goes over in ink.
- Signed-in Amber users see their own fridge, with their real people from
  their own Amber, loaded live.

## Trust, said through objects

- Every note shows how Amber knows it ("from a voice note on 9/14", "from a
  text"), so the privacy story is visible instead of promised.
- A small padlock magnet opens the plain-language privacy promise.

## Paused 2026-10-07 midday: what went wrong and the reset

What kept breaking: every physical thing the browser drew on top of the render (the magnet, the clock, the pen, the ribbon, the leather, a lifted sheet) read as fake next to rendered light, and three agents kept building against a direction that changed underneath them (product, then aura, then no people, then no chrome, then the essay as objects).

The reset, before any more building:
1. Freeze one page of spec for Caleb to approve: the object list, each object's single motion and back, the room's living bits (clock, sky, tree, steam, lamp, fridge door, shelf), the journal, and what's explicitly out.
2. Make motion physical by rendering it. Each interaction (an object sliding out from under its magnet and coming forward, turning over, going back; the fridge opening; the journal lifting; a volume sliding off the shelf; the clock hands) becomes a short Cycles-rendered clip from the same scene and camera, played when tapped. The browser stops drawing physical things entirely. The alternative is real-time 3D in the browser (glTF scene with baked lighting), which allows true dragging but costs a rebuild and loses render quality.
3. Then build in two waves with one agent per wave owning the scene, and a fresh reviewer screenshotting every state before Caleb sees anything.

## Live 3D objects (queued 2026-10-07, for another day)

Caleb asked to make the fridge more than videos and parked it for a later day.

plan keeps everything else and makes only the touched things live.

five door objects and the three pullable journals become real-time 3D in the

   (3 Cycles stills, about 10 min of GPU) to fade to while something is held.

   with the pointer, you flip it yourself, and it falls back onto the door on
   release. A journal slides off the shelf and pages turn under the cursor.
   The essay's click-per-beat flow (`lib/essay.ts`) drives the same objects.
3. Light match (about 2 h). Paper lit to sit in the render: the window's warm
   key, leaf shadows as a projected light cookie that slides across the paper
   as it moves, a soft contact shadow on the fridge door.
4. Phones and ship (about 1 h). Gyro tilt and touch drag, a full screenshot
   pass at 1440 and 390 in every light, deploy to `amber-fridge-draft` only.
