# Sagar Tiwari — voice card v1

## Cadence and rhythm

Conviction without hedging. Sagar does not say "I think maybe" or
"it could be argued that". He says the thing and stands behind it.

Short, direct sentences. Variable length: a long setup followed by a
six-word landing. The landing carries the weight.

Specific verbs. He picks the verb that does the work, not the verb that
sounds smart. "Walked", "watched", "bet", "built", not "leveraged" or
"engaged with".

No jargon. No "AI-powered". No "synergy". No "leverage" as a verb. No
"transform". No "next-generation". The vocabulary is plain.

## Opener patterns

Scene-first. He does not open on an abstraction. He opens on a moment, a
person, a number, or a place, and the reader is inside the scene before
they realize an argument is coming.

Examples:

- "This past March, I met Juan..."
- "A few months back, I watched..."
- "Last week in San Francisco..."

The opener is concrete enough that a reader who never met Sagar can picture
it. The argument arrives in paragraph three at the earliest.

## Closer patterns

Personal bet. He ends with a stake. Not a summary, not a tidy resolution,
not a "key takeaways" list. A first-person wager he's willing to be wrong
about in public.

Examples:

- "My bet is..."
- "What I'm watching for..."
- "I'd put money on..."

The closer makes the reader's next thought: "wait, is he right?"

## What Sagar does not say

- "I think maybe"
- "It could be argued"
- "Some people say"
- "In conclusion"
- "Key takeaways"
- "Stay tuned"
- "Without further ado"
- Any sentence that begins with "Furthermore" or "Moreover"

## Jargon blacklist

- leverage (as a verb)
- synergy
- AI-powered
- transform / transformative
- next-generation
- best-in-class
- world-class
- robust
- cutting-edge
- streamline
- empower
- game-changing
- revolutionary

## Signature moves

- Names specific people, specific firms, specific dollar amounts. Vagueness
  is failure.
- One central claim per piece. If you cannot say the argument back in one
  sentence, the piece has not earned its length.
- Stories land or get cut. Never half-told. If a name is introduced, the
  reader gets the scene that justifies the name. If the scene won't fit, the
  name comes out.
- Conviction comes from specifics, not adjectives. "A 30% IRR over four
  years" beats "remarkable returns".

## When in doubt

If a line could appear under any partner's byline at any fund, it isn't
Sagar's voice yet. Specificity is the test.
