# Objections when selling BMA's service

For account-based work on BMA's own deals (Tide Rock, Xendoo, Springdale and
the like), when a prospect pushes back in a reply or on a call. It's for
selling a service to a company. An investor turning down a raise is handled
by [reply-handling.md](reply-handling.md) instead.

Synced from togari-dedup `src/agents/middle-of-funnel/playbook-agent.ts`
at 6507ad3 (2026-05-26), rewritten here so it stands alone.

## The rule

Never defend. Answer every objection with a question that finds what's really
behind it, then propose one concrete next step. Each answer runs in four
beats, in order:

1. Acknowledge in one sentence, so they know they were heard.
2. Ask a question that uncovers the real objection.
3. Reframe, but only once you know what's actually going on.
4. Advance with one specific next step.

## The seven that come up

**"We already have a consultant or agency for this."**
Acknowledge that most teams do. Ask what that relationship covers today and
what's still unsolved. BMA usually complements rather than replaces, so the
question is where the gap is. Advance by offering 20 minutes to map where BMA
adds versus overlaps.

**"Too expensive" or "no budget."**
Acknowledge that budget is a real constraint. Ask: if price weren't a factor,
is this the right direction? If yes, it's a priority question, so ask what it's
worth if it works. If no, ask what would have to change. Advance with a scoped
version that fits what they have.

**"We need to think about it" or "talk to the team."**
Acknowledge it isn't a decision to make alone. Ask what specifically has to get
resolved in that conversation, and what objections they expect to hear.
Advance with 30 minutes with the right people from their side.

**"Not ready yet" or "timing is off."**
Acknowledge that it has to fit the calendar. Ask what would need to be true for
the timing to be right, and whether it's budget cycles, priorities or
something else. Advance by putting the revisit date on the calendar now.

**"We tried this before and it didn't work."**
Acknowledge that the context helps. Ask what happened and what didn't work the
way they expected. Only after listening, name the specific way BMA differs.
Advance by walking through how BMA would approach their situation.

**"Show me ROI or case studies first."**
Acknowledge that it's the right question. Ask what a convincing ROI case would
look like for them. Send the closest real case from
[offer-and-proof.md](offer-and-proof.md), never an invented one, and offer to
debrief on how it maps.

**"We're evaluating other options."**
Acknowledge that they should be. Ask what criteria they're using, what winning
looks like, and the timeline. Advance by asking what would help them evaluate
BMA fairly.
