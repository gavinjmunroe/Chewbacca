## Mission Statement

**Tizzy uses capture technology to free people from invisible conversational habits they cannot see and therefore cannot change, while maintaining radical respect for privacy, consent, and personal identity.**

We are building assistive technology that empowers growth, not surveillance technology that demands conformity.

---

## The Problem We're Solving

Many autistic people know they are awkward or "missing something" in conversations, but cannot pinpoint what. Parents often feel trapped because they cannot see what their child's school conversations are like, and many kids cannot self-report what went wrong because they did not recognize the social cue in the moment.

**Traditional approaches fail because:**
- Clinic-based therapy doesn't capture real-world patterns
- Self-reporting is unreliable when you can't see the problem
- Video recording is invasive and socially unacceptable
- Generic social skills training doesn't address individual patterns
- Neurotypical coaching often teaches masking, not authentic communication

**Tizzy's approach:**
- Always-present capture reveals real patterns in real contexts
- Audio-first analysis detects "how it was said," not just "what was said"
- Haptic coaching provides gentle guidance in the moment
- Post-conversation review makes invisible patterns visible
- Progress tracking shows measurable improvement over time
- Privacy-first design ensures trust and ethical use

## Core Values

### 1. User Sovereignty

**The user owns their data, their choices, and their identity.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Users can delete any data, anytime, permanently
- Users control what is captured, stored, and shared
- Users choose their coaching philosophy (clarity vs. social calibration)
- Users can disable any feature that doesn't serve them
- Parents have oversight without full surveillance of teens

**What we will never do:**
- Sell user data to third parties
- Share data without explicit consent
- Make privacy controls hidden or confusing
- Guilt users into sharing data
- Create addictive mechanics that prioritize engagement over wellbeing

---

### 2. Clarity Over Conformity

**Tizzy teaches effective communication, not neurotypical performance.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Coaching focuses on "How can you make your intent clear?" not "How can you act normal?"
- We celebrate autistic communication strengths (directness, depth, precision)
- We teach repair and self-advocacy, not masking and suppression
- We help users understand social expectations without demanding they meet them
- We trust users to decide which norms matter to them

**What we will never do:**
- Coach users to "stop being autistic"
- Shame natural autistic communication styles
- Prioritize neurotypical comfort over autistic wellbeing
- Use conformity-based language ("that's weird," "be more normal")
- Treat neurotypical communication as the only valid standard

**Examples of our approach:**

| ❌ Conformity-Based | ✅ Clarity-Based |
|---------------------|------------------|
| "Stop talking about trains, it's boring" | "They changed the topic. Want to check if they want to hear more, or switch topics?" |
| "Make eye contact, it's rude not to" | "They might think you're not listening. Try nodding or saying 'I hear you' to show you are." |
| "Don't be so intense" | "They seem overwhelmed. Try slowing down or taking a breath." |
| "Stop interrupting, it's rude" | "They were still talking. Try waiting 2 seconds to make sure they're done." |

---

### 3. Transparency as Trust

**Privacy is not a policy document. It's a lived experience.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Privacy dashboard shows exactly what data exists, where, and who accessed it
- Consent receipts for every capture session
- Plain-language explanations of what analysis happens where (device vs. cloud)
- Audit logs that users can review
- No hidden data collection, ever

**What we will never do:**
- Bury privacy controls in settings
- Use dark patterns to encourage data sharing
- Update terms to grab more rights without clear notice
- Claim "anonymized" when data is still identifiable
- Treat privacy as legal compliance instead of user respect

**Trust through visibility:**
- Users can see when device is recording (status indicator)
- Users can see what happens to their data (flow diagram)
- Users can see who benefits from their data (transparent business model)
- Users can see changes before they happen (changelog, not surprise updates)

---

### 4. Impact Over Profit

**Tizzy exists to help people, not to extract maximum revenue.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Scholarship pool ensures low-income families can access device
- No selling user data for monetization
- No dark patterns or addictive mechanics
- Product decisions prioritize outcomes over engagement metrics
- We will advocate for policy changes that protect users, even if it's harder for us

**What we will never do:**
- Paywall critical accessibility features
- Create artificial scarcity to drive sales
- Use manipulative pricing (surge pricing, FOMO tactics)
- Optimize for addiction or compulsive use
- Compromise safety or ethics for faster growth

**Business model alignment:**
- Hardware at cost or subsidized (break even or small margin)
- Subscription for coaching service (fair price for ongoing value)
- Institutional licensing (clinics, schools pay for cohort tools)
- Scholarship fund (supported by donors, grants, paying users)

---

### 5. Scientific Rigor and Humility

**We make claims we can prove, and we admit what we don't know.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Clinical validation with speech-language pathologists
- Measurable outcome metrics, not vague promises
- Uncertainty communication ("we think," "this might be," "we're not sure")
- Published research and open collaboration with academics
- Continuous improvement based on evidence, not hype

**What we will never do:**
- Claim diagnostic capability (we are coaching, not assessment)
- Promise unrealistic outcomes ("cure" social difficulties)
- Use testimonials that aren't representative
- Hide negative results or failures
- Overstate AI capabilities (no "magic," just tools)

**Epistemic honesty:**
- When model is uncertain, say so
- When research is preliminary, say so
- When we make mistakes, acknowledge and fix
- When we don't know, admit it and investigate

---

### 6. Co-Design with the Autism Community

**Nothing about us without us.**

**What this means in practice:**
- Advisory board of autistic adults involved in product decisions
- User testing with diverse autistic users throughout development
- Hiring autistic people on product, design, and research teams
- Partnerships with autism advocacy organizations (not just service providers)
- Compensation for community expertise (not just "feedback")

**What we will never do:**
- Design "for" autistic people without autistic voices
- Tokenize autistic advisors (actual influence, not optics)
- Prioritize parent/therapist preferences over autistic user needs
- Assume all autistic people want the same things
- Ignore criticism from the autism community

**Whose voice matters most:**
- For adult users: the users themselves
- For minor users: the user's voice, with parent partnership
- For product decisions: autistic advisors have veto power on harmful features
- For research: community consent and benefit-sharing

## Product Principles

### Principle 1: The Daily Loop Must Close

**Every feature must serve the core loop:** Capture → Coaching → Review → Progress → Improvement

**What this means:**
- No "cool tech" features that don't drive outcomes
- Focus beats breadth (V1 is narrow and effective, not broad and mediocre)
- Measure success by user improvement, not feature count
- If a feature doesn't close the loop, it's V2 or cut

**Anti-pattern:** Humane AI Pin tried to be "the future" without a clear daily value loop. Result: shutdown risk and poor adoption.

---

### Principle 2: Privacy is a Feature, Not a Constraint

**Privacy controls are not obstacles to overcome. They are core product value.**

**What this means:**
- Privacy dashboard is a first-class feature, not buried in settings
- Consent mechanisms are simple and empowering, not friction
- Local processing is a selling point (data stays on your device)
- Transparency builds trust, which drives adoption

**Anti-pattern:** Friend pendant treated privacy as a legal checkbox. Result: social discomfort and backlash.

---

### Principle 3: Graceful Degradation Over Perfect Accuracy

**When uncertain, be honest. Don't hallucinate confidence.**

**What this means:**
- Conservative confidence thresholds for real-time coaching
- Post-conversation review can say "we're not sure, what do you think?"
- Personalization improves accuracy over time (learn the user)
- User feedback loop improves model (false positives get downweighted)

**Anti-pattern:** Overconfident AI systems that give bad advice with high certainty destroy trust faster than admitting uncertainty.

---

### Principle 4: Social Acceptance is a Design Problem, Not a Marketing Problem

**The device must be socially legible as assistive technology.**

**What this means:**
- Design for discretion, not covertness (there's a difference)
- Consent mechanism is simple and socially comfortable
- Bystander experience matters (they need to feel safe too)
- The device should not increase stigma for the user

**Anti-pattern:** "Just hide the device" assumes bystander concerns don't matter. They do.

---

### Principle 5: Coaching is Scaffolding, Not Dependency

**Tizzy is training wheels, not a permanent crutch.**

**What this means:**
- Goal is internalization: user learns patterns and becomes independent
- Haptic coaching can fade as skills improve
- Progress metrics celebrate growth toward independence
- "Graduation" is celebrated, not feared

**Anti-pattern:** Addiction-driven products want users dependent forever. We want users to outgrow us.

---

### Principle 6: Context Beats Universals

**Good communication is context-dependent. We personalize, not standardize.**

**What this means:**
- User's baseline matters more than population norms
- Cultural context affects communication norms
- Relationship context affects appropriate behavior
- Environment context affects what's possible (quiet library vs. loud cafeteria)

**Anti-pattern:** One-size-fits-all social skills training that ignores context creates robotic, inauthentic communication.

## Design Guidelines

### User Interface Principles

1. **Clarity over cleverness**
   - Plain language, no jargon
   - Visual hierarchy that guides attention
   - Information when you need it, hidden when you don't

2. **Empowerment over paternalism**
   - Give users control, don't decide for them
   - Offer suggestions, not commands
   - Explain the "why" behind coaching

3. **Celebration over criticism**
   - Lead with "what went well"
   - Frame improvement as growth, not failure
   - Progress metrics show trajectory, not judgment

4. **Simplicity over comprehensiveness**
   - One micro-goal per day, not a overwhelming list
   - Top 2-3 insights per conversation, not everything detected
   - Clear next actions, not analysis paralysis

---

### Coaching Language Principles

1. **Observation, not judgment**
   - ✅ "They changed the topic"
   - ❌ "You were boring them"

2. **Curiosity, not certainty**
   - ✅ "This might have been confusing for them"
   - ❌ "You confused them"

3. **Options, not commands**
   - ✅ "You could try asking a return question"
   - ❌ "Ask a return question"

4. **Strengths-based framing**
   - ✅ "You're great at depth. Here's how to invite others in."
   - ❌ "You talk too much about your interests"

5. **Contextual, not universal**
   - ✅ "In this situation, they seemed to want..."
   - ❌ "People always expect..."

---

### Privacy Design Principles

1. **Visible by default**
   - Privacy controls are prominent, not buried
   - Status is always clear (recording/muted/off)
   - Data flow diagrams available to all users

2. **Consent is easy and explicit**
   - One-tap privacy mode switching
   - Clear language about what each mode means
   - Bystander consent flows are simple and socially comfortable

3. **Deletion is real**
   - "Delete" means "permanently gone," not "hidden"
   - No sneaky backups or shadow copies
   - Audit log shows deletion happened

4. **Minimization by design**
   - Only capture what's needed
   - Only store what's valuable
   - Only share what's consented
   - Default to most private option

## Decision Framework

When making product decisions, we ask:

1. **Does this serve the daily loop?** (Capture → Coach → Review → Progress)
2. **Does this respect user sovereignty?** (Control, transparency, consent)
3. **Does this teach clarity or conformity?** (Must be clarity)
4. **Does this build or erode trust?** (Trust is non-negotiable)
5. **Is this scientifically sound?** (Evidence-based, measurable)
6. **Would the autism community approve?** (Co-design, not design-for)

If the answer to any question is "no," we either redesign or cut the feature.

## Anti-Patterns We Avoid

### Product Anti-Patterns
- ❌ **Feature bloat:** Trying to be everything at once (V1 is focused)
- ❌ **Vanity metrics:** Optimizing for engagement over outcomes
- ❌ **Dark patterns:** Manipulating users into sharing more data
- ❌ **Surveillance creep:** Adding tracking features "because we can"

### Business Anti-Patterns
- ❌ **Data monetization:** Selling user data to third parties
- ❌ **Planned obsolescence:** Breaking old devices to force upgrades
- ❌ **Exclusionary pricing:** Making product inaccessible to those who need it most
- ❌ **Investor appeasement:** Compromising values for faster growth

### Technology Anti-Patterns
- ❌ **Overconfident AI:** Hallucinating certainty when model is uncertain
- ❌ **Black box systems:** No explainability or auditability
- ❌ **Vendor lock-in:** Dependency on single provider with no exit plan
- ❌ **Security by obscurity:** Hoping problems aren't discovered instead of fixing them

### Social Anti-Patterns
- ❌ **Savior complex:** Treating autistic people as "broken" and needing "fixing"
- ❌ **Tokenism:** Having autistic advisors for optics without real influence
- ❌ **Parent-centric design:** Prioritizing parent control over user autonomy
- ❌ **Medical model:** Treating autism as disease instead of neurological difference

## Ruthless Prioritization Statement

**Not everything that matters makes it into V1.**

We prioritize:
- ✅ Core daily loop over nice-to-have features
- ✅ Trust and privacy over convenience
- ✅ Accuracy and safety over speed to market
- ✅ Autism wedge over general market breadth
- ✅ Outcomes over engagement metrics
- ✅ Scientific validation over hype

We defer to V2:
- ⏸️ Video/facial expression analysis
- ⏸️ Multi-device ecosystem
- ⏸️ Therapist dashboard (B2B portal)
- ⏸️ School integration
- ⏸️ Advanced social calibration
- ⏸️ API for third-party integrations

**Why:** Shipping focused value now beats promising comprehensive value later. Humane AI Pin tried to be the future. Ray-Ban Meta shipped clear utility. Be Ray-Ban Meta, not Humane.

## Final Word: Why This Matters

**Autistic people deserve tools that empower, not oppress.**

For too long, social skills interventions have focused on conformity: teaching autistic people to act neurotypical, suppress their natural communication, and mask their identity. This approach causes burnout, anxiety, and loss of self.

**Tizzy offers a different path:** Understand the patterns holding you back, learn to communicate clearly while being yourself, and build skills that honor who you are.

This is hard to build. It requires technology, clinical expertise, community partnership, and deep ethical commitment. But it's worth building.

**Because every person deserves to be heard, understood, and empowered to connect with others in their own authentic way.**

That's why Tizzy exists.
