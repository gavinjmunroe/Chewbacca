# Demand-gen practitioner rules, 2025-2026

Researched 2026-10-07, with a source after every rule. Vendor benchmarks (Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist) are self-reported platform data with a sales motive. Treat them as directional.

## ColdIQ (Michel Lieben)

- Lists of 500 to 1,000 contacts with one play each. Claimed 20-30% replies against 2-3% on 100k blasts. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147, 2026-10-06; the X fetch returned 402, so these numbers come from the saved summary in second-brain `reference_lieben_cold_email_playbook`)
- Max 30 sends per inbox per day, ramped 10, then 20, then 30. Bounces under 3%. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Email 1 is plain text, no links, tracking off. He cites Hunter: +68% replies. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- An offer CTA beats a meeting ask. He cites Gong: +28% for offers, -44% for meeting asks. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- 3-step sequence, with steps 2 and 3 sent with an empty subject so they thread. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Tier accounts by lookalike-to-closed-won. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Run LinkedIn ads at target accounts 1-2 weeks before the cold email goes out, then email the accounts that engaged. (https://kokasexton.com/insights/coldlabs.html, a secondhand summary)
- On LinkedIn: 200-300 touches a week aimed at people who engaged with a post recently, soft CTAs ("Want the link?"), voice notes capped at 25s. A third party reports 32% replies on 1,400+ messages. (https://the-linkedin-lead-machine.beehiiv.com/p/the-michel-lieben-cold-outreach-os-for-2025-26, unverified against Lieben)

## Growth Engine X (Eric Nowoslawski)

- Cold outbound fits a TAM over 100,000 and an LTV over $10,000. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- His best play is "creative ideas": AI writes 3 specific ways the product helps this prospect, drawn from preset value props and never invented freely. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- The offer has to be something competitors can't say, and so strong the prospect would pay for the call. Example: free test campaigns, or payment only after a bad review is removed, aimed at businesses rated 3.5-4.5 stars. Result: 1 positive reply per 70 emails. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- Emails stay under 100 words. Email 1 sells the reply and never the product. Use soft CTAs. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Kill a campaign after 500-1,000 sends with no traction. Read a low open rate as spam placement and leave the subject line alone. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Over 3% bounce means stop and re-validate. Recycle a list after 3 months, 2 at minimum. Keep a warmed backup set of inboxes equal to live capacity. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Run dozens of campaigns in parallel. Expect 30-60 days of warmup and testing before full stride. Email waterfall order: Leadmagic, Prospeo, GetProspect, Icypeas, TryKitt. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)

## Leadbird (Nick Abraham)

- Infrastructure split: 40% Google, 40% Microsoft, 20% SMTP. Move to premium infrastructure at 40-50k emails a month. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Run two full inbox sets and swap them month on month, which gives each set a 45-day warmup cycle. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- When sender bounces pass 1-3%, the domain is used up and gets rotated. Send to hard gateways (Barracuda, Proofpoint) last, once domains have aged. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Keep it under 100 words, since fatigue sets in past 120. Personalize one variable per email and never let AI write the whole body. Strong variables: a colleague's name ("you or Liam?"), the prospect's own ICP, a competitor list. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Floor is 1% reply rate, target 2-4%. 20-40% of replies should be positive. Expect 1 qualified lead per 350-500 contacts. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Scale: 800+ meetings a month across 120+ clients, run by about 10 people. (https://smartlead.ai/blog/case-study-leadbird-by-nick-abraham)

## Clay and Clay University

- Clay 101 teaches FETE: Find, Enrich, Transform, Export. (https://university.clay.com/courses/clay-101)
- Waterfall enrichment lifts coverage from about 30% to 80%+. One five-tool personal email test reached 79%. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)
- Score tier-one accounts on fit, engagement and contract value. Measure meetings booked, hours saved, and trial conversion. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)
- First-party signals (CRM notes, replies, call transcripts) are the moat, and rented signals are not. No blast-all sends. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)

## Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist benchmarks

- Instantly 2026, covering calendar 2025: average reply 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%+, top 10% at 10.7%+. Below 2% means something is broken. 58% of replies come from step 1. (https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- Instantly: 4-7 steps, under 80 words, follow-ups at 2-3 days then 4-7, warmup 2-4 weeks, bounce under 2%, about $153 per meeting. The same page also calls 3 emails optimal and puts the safe ceiling near 100 sends per warmed inbox per day. (https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- Smartlead Q1-Q3 2025, 3.2M emails: inbox placement about 73% by Q3, with Gmail the best. 50-80 words got a 9.1% reply. Mid-week sends do 22% better. The 3rd follow-up hit 7.4% reply against 3.2% for the 1st. (https://marketing-assets.smartlead.ai/guides/Cold_Email_Blueprint_Q1_Q3_2025.pdf)
- Lemlist's own roundup: Belkins measured 0.45% on 7.5M emails under strict counting. Meeting rate runs 0.5-2.5%. Spam complaints must stay under 0.3%. SaaS often lands under 2% reply. (https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)
- Lemlist's "17% reply on image-personalized sequences" and "2.8x lift from video" circulate on third-party sites, and I could not trace either to a primary Lemlist source. **Unverified.**

## Jordan Crawford (Blueprint GTM)

- PQS, the pain-qualified segment: public data proves the pain before the first email. Job titles never prove it. The pain should be new, existential, and findable in data. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/, https://cannonballgtm.substack.com/p/the-cannonball-gtm-glossary-essential?r=56vz7i)
- PVP, the permissionless value prop: send output the prospect would pay for before asking for anything. (https://www.joinpavilion.com/blog/the-man-who-mastered-ai-gtm-meet-jordan-crawford)
- Pain sources: live job descriptions, tech installed last week, what a funding release says the money is for, competitor reviews naming missing features, regulatory changes. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)
- 50 companies matching a sharp heuristic beat 5,000 generic ones. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)
- Get to the pain in two sentences, with no scrolling on a phone. Subject lines read like an internal note. CTA is a low-friction question that confirms the pain, never a 15-minute ask. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)

## Kyle Coleman (ex-Looker, Clari, Copy.ai CMO)

- 72 words max in his formula. Hard ceiling of 125 words or one scroll. One idea per line. (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman, https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyletcoleman_cold-email-101-activity-6899031112140554240-4NQd)
- Structure: a trigger as the intro, a body tying the trigger to the persona's outcome, then the CTA. (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman)
- Interest CTAs ("Curious to learn more?") with "Either way, thanks for reading." (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman)
- Never open with "Based on your title," "I saw your profile," or "My company does." (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyletcoleman_sdr-sales-activity-6925131006093975552-Tc-4)

## Josh Braun

- 4T: Truth (an observable fact), Think (a neutral problem observation), Third-party proof, Talk (a low-friction ask). Under 80 words. (https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method, a third-party summary)
- "Poke the bear": one question that surfaces a problem the prospect can't see, with no pitch. (https://somethinginc.com/blog/poke-the-bear-vs-ai-volume-outbound-debate/)
- CTAs: "Would it be worth a quick look?", "Feel free to say no." The subject line names the truth ("Your SDR expansion"). (https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method)

## Where they agree

- Short copy: 50-100 words, with Coleman at 72 and Braun and Instantly under 80.
- Plain text, a conversational tone, and no links or tracking in email 1.
- A soft, interest-based, or offer CTA beats asking for a meeting.
- Narrow, signal- or pain-defined segments beat big lists.
- Bounces under 2-3%, rotated domains, backup inboxes, and a warmup of 2 to 6 weeks.
- Measure positive replies and meetings, not opens.
- Personalize with one relevant variable. A whole AI-written email or a flattering first line doesn't count.

## Where they disagree

- **Sends per inbox per day:** Lieben caps at 30 and the general consensus is 25-30. Instantly's page says about 100 is safe.
- **Sequence length:** Lieben runs 3 steps. Instantly and Braun say 4-7, while Instantly's same page also calls 3 optimal.
- **Where replies come from:** Instantly puts 58% on step 1. Smartlead shows the 3rd follow-up at more than double step 1's reply rate.
- **List size:** Crawford says 50 companies. Lieben says 500-1,000. Nowoslawski kills a campaign only after 500-1,000 sends and wants a TAM over 100k.
- **What a "good" reply rate is:** Abraham 2-4%, Instantly average 3.43%, Belkins 0.45%, Lieben 20-30%. Each counts differently.
- **Personalization:** Nowoslawski puts offer above personalization. Lemlist's unverified figures say heavy visual personalization wins. Crawford calls AI first lines a party trick.
- **Channel:** ColdIQ warms accounts with ads and LinkedIn first. Leadbird and Growth Engine X are email-volume shops.
