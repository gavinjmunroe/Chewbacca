# Product Vision: Tizzy

**Working Tagline:** The conversation coach you can actually trust.

---

## What Tizzy Is

Tizzy is an always-wearable conversation coach, starting with autism. The core product is a necklace (or similarly discreet wearable) that captures real-world conversations, analyzes the acoustic and social signals that make conversations succeed or fail, and turns that into coaching that is:

1. **Subtle in the moment** - haptic feedback
2. **Clear right after** - post-conversation review
3. **Measurable over weeks** - trend dashboards

### Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision is bigger than autism: Tizzy becomes the default platform for conversational growth across:

- Speech development
- Social communication
- Leadership communication
- Interview preparation
- Therapy applications
- Relationship skills

**But autism is the strategic wedge** because it is:
- Underserved
- High-need
- Dataset moat is real

---

## What Tizzy Is NOT

### Not an "AI Friend" or Companionship Device

That category is already getting public backlash because it feels like surveillance and because the product value is unclear. The Friend pendant is the cautionary tale here:

- Always listening
- Socially uncomfortable
- Made bystanders uneasy
- WIRED's review describes the social reaction, including people suspecting the wearer is "wearing a wire"

**Tizzy cannot be perceived as creepy tech.** It has to feel like:
- Assistive technology with a legitimate mission
- Visible governance
- User control that is real

### Not a Medical Device in V1

In V1, Tizzy is coaching and self-improvement tooling, with optional clinician collaboration.

If you later want clinical claims or clinical decision support, you build a parallel regulatory track and do validation studies deliberately.

## Product Philosophy

### Core Statement

Tizzy uses capture technology to free people from invisible conversational habits they cannot see and therefore cannot change, while maintaining radical respect for privacy, consent, and personal identity.

### Operational Rules

1. **The user is never punished for being autistic**
2. **The user is coached toward understanding and clarity, not conformity**
3. **The user is always in control of capture, retention, and sharing**
4. **Trust is a feature, not a policy document**

---

## The Tizzy Trust Model

Nobody believes always-on devices. So you build a system that is auditable and boringly transparent.

### Core Trust Commitments

1. **Default: local processing and minimization**
   - Raw audio is processed on-device into feature representations
   - Raw audio is not retained unless explicitly enabled per user and per context

2. **Consent receipts**
   - Every capture session has a record of whether it was "self-only," "consented conversation," or "no capture"

3. **Bystander protection**
   - The system is designed to focus on the wearer's behavior
   - If you ever store bystander voice, it is explicitly consented, and there is a deletion pathway

4. **Hard mute and "quiet hours"**
   - Mandatory user control features

5. **A visible, socially acceptable indicator when recording other people**
   - Not an obnoxious LED, but something that meets the same goal
   - Bystanders can reasonably know when capture is happening

6. **No clip sharing virality**
   - Only therapist or trusted guardian sharing
   - Only with explicit opt-in

**This is the difference between "assistive tech" and "surveillance necklace."**

## Execution Roadmap

### 0-90 Days: Build the Loop

- Prototype necklace hardware (or dev kit form factor)
- Build capture pipeline
- Ship app with post-conversation review
- Ship haptics MVP
- Recruit 20-50 adult autistic beta users (college wedge)

### 3-6 Months: Prove Value

- Demonstrate 30-day improvement metrics for at least 2 coaching targets
  - Topic maintenance
  - Repair
  - Reciprocity
- Build therapist export
- Build privacy dashboard

### 6-12 Months: Build Moat

- Scale dataset collection ethically
- Expand coaching taxonomy
- Improve personalization
- Begin institutional partnerships with clinics

### 12-24 Months: Institutional Expansion

- Schools only after governance and compliance posture is strong and validated

## Core Decisions to Be Made

These are not optional questions. They are core decisions that determine whether Tizzy becomes a world-changing assistive product or a scandal.

1. **Exact consent mechanism design** for bystanders, especially in mixed environments
2. **Default retention period** and whether raw audio ever leaves device by default
3. **Whether "self-only" mode** (capturing only wearer voice) is viable as a default safety mode
4. **The line between coaching and clinical claims**
5. **The Modulate contract boundary** around training rights and data usage
6. **The minimum viable school compliance package** (if you ever go there)

## Final Ruthless Positioning Statement

**Tizzy wins if and only if it becomes the first always-wearable conversational coach that is both effective and trusted.**

The category is heating up fast:
- Amazon moving on Bee confirms the space is real
- Backlash against creepy "AI friend" wearables confirms trust is the differentiator

### Your Unfair Advantage

Your unfair advantage is NOT that you thought of the idea.

Your unfair advantage is that you are:
1. **Building the autism wedge from lived experience**
2. **Designing coaching that is humane** instead of conformity-driven
3. **Treating privacy and consent as the product**, not as legal fine print

## The Hardest Problems

### Problem 1: Social Acceptance and Bystander Trust

Friend triggered bystander discomfort. If you think you can hide the device and avoid the issue, you are misunderstanding the issue.

**The issue is not visibility, it is legitimacy. You must build legitimacy.**

### Problem 2: Minors, Consent, and Schools

COPPA, state recording laws, school policies, FERPA vendor control. This is not a quick hurdle. It is a core design requirement.

### Problem 3: Accuracy and Harm Risk

If you buzz a kid incorrectly in a conversation, you can worsen anxiety and social performance. You need uncertainty handling and personalization.

### Problem 4: "Masking" Trap

Tizzy must not teach people to become fake. It must teach:
- Clarity
- Repair
- Boundaries
- Self-advocacy

**The product philosophy must explicitly prefer healthy communication over social conformity.**

### Problem 5: Hardware Audio Quality Under Clothing

Necklace-under-shirt is convenient but acoustically challenging. You will need strong signal processing and potentially alternate wear options.
