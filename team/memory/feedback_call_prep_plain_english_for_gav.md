Call prep docs that Gavin reads during a call are written in plain English: full words (you, your, with, about), no u/ur/w/rn/abt, and every insider term explained once (MSP = IT support company, SBIR = federal research grant). Each page names who says each step (Gavin or Caleb).

**Why:** Gavin, 2026-10-28: "I couldn't understand it on the fly. Yesterdays was mid. Send me call prep in English pls." The 10/27 prep was in Caleb's texting register.

**How to apply:** Caleb's texting voice is for chat with Caleb only. Anything a teammate or client reads mid-call uses standard English, one page per call. Related: [[feedback_let_gav_lead_sales_calls]].

Format, since 2026-10-29: a live call doc has three kinds of line and each looks different. Words to say out loud are black, in quotes, one short sentence per line. Things to DO (share screen, open a tab, paste a link) are bold blue. The speaker (GAV, CALEB) is small gray caps above their lines. Don't-say and backup links go last in small gray. No paragraphs, no explanation of why. Build it as HTML with inline colors and upload to Drive as text/html so the colors survive. Caleb, 2026-10-29, on a markdown run sheet: "the whole doc is so hard to follow I cant speak and interpret at the same time... make actions a diff color". Example: Doc 14wG5iYMBXzGwYNRJxjqcSD7HSdhLlH_LFY73e1YMam4 (John Knox call).

Sheets go by link, never screen share (Caleb, 2026-10-29: "When we do google sheets we'll just give him the link so we don't needa share the screen"). The call doc says paste the link in the chat, then walks the person through the tabs by voice.
