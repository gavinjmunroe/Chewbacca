# Tizzy — Product Overview

## Executive Summary

Tizzy is an always-wearable conversation coach built autism-first. It ships as a discreet pendant that listens passively during real conversations, delivers real-time haptic guidance, and surfaces a post-conversation replay and skill-trend dashboard on a paired iPhone. The core promise: you leave every conversation slightly better than you entered it — and you can prove it over time.

Tizzy is not a chatbot. It is not a companion AI. It is a coach — present in the moment, invisible to others, and relentlessly focused on one thing: helping autistic people develop genuine conversation skills that generalize to the real world.

---

## What Tizzy Is

**An always-wearable conversation coach.**
You put it on. You forget it is there. It is listening. After the conversation ends, it tells you what happened and what to work on next. Over weeks and months it shows you how you are improving across a specific, measurable taxonomy of social communication skills.

**A haptic language.**
The coach speaks to you through three vibration patterns delivered silently to your collarbone or sternum. No screen to check. No earpiece that stigmatizes. Just a private signal that arrives at the exact moment it matters.

**A closed feedback loop.**
Real coaching requires: (1) intervention in the moment, (2) post-session review, and (3) long-term trend tracking. Tizzy delivers all three. The moment → the replay → the arc.

**A privacy-first device.**
All audio processing happens locally on-device by default. No clips are stored in the cloud. No conversation is ever shared without explicit opt-in. The pendant has a hard-mute physical button and a visible indicator LED. Bystander consent is taken seriously, not dismissed.

---

## What Tizzy Is NOT

**Not a medical device (V1).**
Tizzy is a coaching tool, not a diagnostic instrument. It does not diagnose autism, anxiety, or any other condition. It does not claim to treat or cure. V1 ships as a consumer wellness device with coaching functionality.

**Not an AI friend or companion.**
The Friend pendant (and its successors) sell you a parasocial relationship with an AI. Tizzy sells you a skill. The distinction is enormous. Tizzy's success metric is that you need it less over time — because you have internalized the coaching.

**Not a surveillance tool.**
Tizzy is not for parents to monitor their children. It is not for employers. It is not for therapists to access without user consent. The data belongs to the user. Full stop.

**Not a cure.**
Autism is not a disease. Tizzy is not a fix. It is a scaffold — the same way a tennis coach or a language tutor is a scaffold. It meets you where you are and helps you get where you want to go, on your own terms.

## The Product Mission

**Mission:** Give autistic people a way to get honest, private, real-time feedback on real conversations — and accumulate evidence of their own growth.

The deeper purpose underneath the mission: most conversation coaching for autistic people happens in clinical offices, far removed from the actual social situations that matter. Role-play in a therapy room does not automatically transfer to a job interview, a college orientation, or a first date. Tizzy puts the coach in the room.

---

## The Wedge: Autism During Transitions

The initial beachhead is not all autistic people. It is autistic people navigating major life transitions — primarily the college transition (ages 17–22), where social demands spike precisely when support systems disappear.

Why this wedge:
- Autistic college students are underserved and have acute unmet need
- They are motivated to improve (career stakes, social stakes)
- They have smartphones and tolerate novel technology
- The transition period is time-bounded, creating natural urgency
- University disability services (OSAS equivalents) are a distribution channel
- The clinical evidence base (SLP + CBT + behavioral coaching) already exists — Tizzy operationalizes it

The wedge is not the ceiling. After proving the product in college transitions, Tizzy expands to adult professional settings, then to adolescents via therapist channels, then (carefully, with governance) to schools.

## The 30-Day Value Promise

In 30 days of consistent use, a Tizzy user will be able to:

1. **Name their top two conversation patterns** (e.g., "I interrupt when anxious" or "I trail off before finishing thoughts")
2. **Identify one measurable improvement** in one of those patterns versus their baseline week
3. **Point to at least three specific conversation moments** where the haptic cue helped them make a different choice in real time

The 30-day value promise is not "you will be a great conversationalist." It is: "You will know yourself better as a communicator, and you will have evidence that you can change."

That evidence is the product's moat. Once a user has 30 days of their own data showing real growth, they will not switch to a competitor that offers no such continuity.
