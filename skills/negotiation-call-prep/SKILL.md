# Negotiation call prep

A doc the user reads mid-call, in call order, built to win a number.

## First step, before writing a line

1. Find EVERY prior doc on this negotiation: `ls ~/Downloads | grep -i <topic>`,
   the memory note, past sessions (`grep -l -i <topic> ~/.claude/projects/*/*.jsonl`).
   Read each one in full.
2. List every lever and technique they name. The new doc carries ALL of them
   unless the user dropped one. A rewrite that loses a lever is a regression.
3. Fold in the newest facts (latest email or quote) over older notes.

## What the doc must contain

- Goal number, walk-away number, and the other side's exact current offer
- Questions that make them talk first
- Every lever you bring: the whole package, multi-year, sign speed, lower
  cost to serve (e.g. people gone weekends), referrals
- Their own numbers as anchors (their site, comps they can verify)
- The vacancy or loss math in their terms: what a no costs THEM
- Technique lines, written as words to say: small yeses first, accusation
  audit, labels ("sounds like your hands are tied") then silence, mirroring,
  how-questions not why, concession steps that each get something back
- Non-rent asks if the price won't move, a deadline line, and a written close
- A never-say list: anyone's max, "in at any price", claims you can't back

## Never

- Never build from just one prior file. On 2026-10-28, five minutes before
  a housing call, the doc dropped levers from the group one-pager. The user:
  "didn't include a bunch of the persuasion things and techniques we said
  we'd use". Then: "Max out the persuasion and argument building bro".
  Ship it maxed out the FIRST time.
- Never put in a comp or claim with no source. Say so if one is missing.
