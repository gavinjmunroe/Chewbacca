# Partnership Strategy: Therapy Clinics, Schools, & Institutions

**Document Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** February 13, 2026
**Owner:** Business Development & Sales Teams

---

## Executive Summary

Tizzy's partnership strategy is designed to build **credibility, distribution, and dataset moat** through strategic relationships with therapy clinics, schools, and autism-focused institutions. These partnerships are not just sales channels—they are **trust validators, data contributors, and outcome proof points.**

**Three Partnership Pillars:**
1. **Speech-Language Pathology (SLP) Clinics:** Clinical validation, therapy referrals, real-world outcome data
2. **Schools & Universities:** Institutional scale, transition support (middle school, high school, college)
3. **Autism Organizations & Research Institutions:** Advocacy, research collaboration, community credibility

**Strategic Goals:**
- Year 1: Sign 10 SLP clinic partnerships
- Year 2: Expand to 50 therapy partners + 3 institutional pilots
- Year 3: 150 therapy partners + 10 school/university contracts

---

## Partnership Pillar 1: Speech-Language Pathology Clinics

### Why SLP Clinics are Critical

**Clinical Credibility:**
- SLPs are the trusted authority for parents seeking social communication support
- Therapist recommendation = immediate trust and higher conversion
- Clinical validation = publishable research, peer-reviewed outcomes

**Distribution Channel:**
- SLPs see 5-20 autistic clients per week (captive, high-intent audience)
- Direct recommendation = low CAC ($50-75 vs. $200-300 for cold ads)
- Recurring referrals = sustainable growth pipeline

**Dataset Growth:**
- Therapy-connected users = higher-quality labels (therapist annotations)
- Structured outcome tracking (ASHA benchmarks, pre/post assessments)
- Consent rates higher (therapist-mediated, clear value exchange)

---

### Target Clinic Profiles

**Tier 1 (High Priority):**
- Private SLP practices specializing in autism (5-20 therapists)
- Strong local reputation, active parent referral network
- Tech-forward (already use apps, digital tools, outcome tracking)
- Located in metro areas (higher autism diagnosis rates, affluent clientele)

**Examples:**
- Pediatric speech clinics (Boston, San Francisco, Seattle, Austin)
- Autism centers with in-house SLP teams (e.g., Gateway Learning Group, The Stepping Stones Group)

**Tier 2 (Medium Priority):**
- Hospital-based SLP departments (outpatient autism programs)
- University speech-language clinics (research collaboration potential)
- Multi-location therapy franchises (scale potential, but slower decision-making)

**Tier 3 (Lower Priority, Year 2+):**
- School-based SLP services (FERPA complexity, procurement cycles)
- Teletherapy platforms (Expressable, ConnectAbility—potential white-label)

---

### Partnership Value Proposition for Clinics

**For SLPs:**
- "Finally see what happens outside your office—real-world topic maintenance, repair, reciprocity."
- "Turn 1 hour of weekly therapy into 24/7 coaching that extends your impact."
- "Measurable outcomes, publishable data, differentiation from competitors."

**For Clinic Owners:**
- "Increase client retention and referrals with cutting-edge assistive technology."
- "Co-marketing: position your clinic as early adopter and innovation leader."
- "Revenue share or referral fees (optional): earn 10-20% on Tizzy sales to your clients."

**For Parents (via Clinic):**
- "Your therapist recommends Tizzy as a way to extend therapy into daily life."
- "Real-world data helps your therapist tailor sessions to your child's actual challenges."
- "Measurable progress, not guesswork."

---

### Partnership Models (3 Options)

#### Model A: Referral Partner (Easiest Entry)

**Structure:**
- Clinic recommends Tizzy to clients (no financial commitment)
- Parents purchase Tizzy directly (consumer pricing: $300 hardware + $40/month)
- Clinic receives 10-20% referral commission on first-year subscriptions

**Clinic Benefits:**
- Zero upfront cost, low risk
- Additional revenue stream ($50-100 per referred client)
- Improved client outcomes (better retention, referrals)

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Warm leads (40-50% conversion vs. 5-10% cold traffic)
- Clinical endorsement (trust signal)
- Scalable (can sign 50+ clinics quickly)

**Best For:** Small private practices, pilot phase

---

#### Model B: Clinical License Partner (Deep Integration)

**Structure:**
- Clinic pays annual license ($1,000/year per SLP)
- Includes therapist dashboard, training, cohort analytics, priority support
- Clinic bulk-purchases devices for clients (subsidized: $200-250 per device)
- Clients pay subscription directly to Tizzy ($30-40/month)

**Clinic Benefits:**
- Dashboard access to client real-world data (with consent)
- Differentiation ("We use cutting-edge Tizzy technology")
- Co-marketing (case studies, conference presentations)
- CEU-eligible training

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Predictable B2B revenue ($1K per therapist annually)
- Deeper data integration (therapist annotations, outcome tracking)
- Stronger partnership (financial commitment = alignment)

**Best For:** Established clinics (10+ therapists), autism-specialized centers

---

#### Model C: Research Collaboration Partner (Outcome Validation)

**Structure:**
- Clinic enrolls clients into IRB-approved research study
- Tizzy provides devices free or subsidized (research budget)
- Clinic collects pre/post outcome data (ASHA benchmarks, validated scales)
- Co-author published research (peer-reviewed journals, conference presentations)

**Clinic Benefits:**
- Publish research (professional development, credibility)
- Free/subsidized devices for clients (recruiting incentive)
- Access to cutting-edge technology pre-launch

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Clinical validation (gold standard evidence)
- High-quality labeled dataset (expert annotations)
- Publications = credibility for institutional sales

**Best For:** University clinics, research-focused practices, Year 1-2 pilots

### Partnership Acquisition Playbook

#### Step 1: Identify Target Clinics (Week 1-2)

**Data Sources:**
- ASHA's Find a Professional directory (filter: autism, social communication)
- Autism Speaks Resource Directory (therapy providers by region)
- Local autism parent groups (Facebook, Reddit—ask for recommendations)
- Google Maps ("speech therapy autism near [city]")

**Target List:**
- Create spreadsheet with 100 high-priority clinics
- Prioritize by: size, autism focus, tech adoption, location

---

#### Step 2: Outreach & Initial Contact (Week 3-4)

**Outreach Channels:**
1. **Email (Primary):** Personalized email to clinic director or lead SLP
2. **LinkedIn:** Connect with clinic owners, SLP influencers
3. **Phone:** Follow-up call if no email response within 7 days
4. **Conferences:** ASHA annual conference, state SLP events (booth or networking)

**Email Template (Initial Outreach):**

**Subject:** Helping your autistic clients generalize therapy to real life

Hi [SLP Name],

I'm reaching out because [Clinic Name] is doing incredible work supporting autistic clients with social communication challenges. I'm the founder of Tizzy, a wearable conversation coach designed to extend your therapy impact into daily life.

**The problem we hear from SLPs:** Clinic progress doesn't always translate to real-world conversations. Parents ask, "Why is my child doing well in therapy but struggling at school?"

**What Tizzy does:** It captures real-world conversations, analyzes acoustic and social patterns (prosody, turn-taking, topic maintenance), and provides in-the-moment coaching via haptics. After each conversation, users get actionable insights. You get a dashboard showing real-world patterns (with client consent).

**We're looking for 10 early partner clinics** to pilot Tizzy and co-validate outcomes. In exchange:
- Free therapist dashboard access (Year 1)
- Subsidized devices for your clients
- Co-marketing and case study opportunities
- CEU-eligible training

Would you be open to a 20-minute call to explore whether this could help your clients?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Tizzy Website Link]

---

#### Step 3: Discovery Call (Week 5-6)

**Call Objectives:**
1. Understand clinic pain points (generalization, parent communication, outcome tracking)
2. Present Tizzy value proposition (demo video if available)
3. Address concerns (privacy, cost, complexity)
4. Propose partnership model (referral, license, or research)
5. Get commitment for next step (pilot with 3-5 clients, formal agreement)

**Key Questions to Ask:**
- "How do you currently help clients generalize therapy to real-world settings?"
- "What do parents most often ask for or struggle with?"
- "How do you track outcomes beyond in-clinic assessments?"
- "Would you be interested in seeing real-world data from your clients' conversations?"

**Objection Handling:**

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "My clients can't afford $300 + subscription." | "We offer scholarships and sliding scale. Also, devices can be subsidized for data donors." |
| "Privacy concerns—parents won't want recording." | "Privacy is our #1 design principle. Default is on-device processing, no raw audio retention unless explicitly enabled. Parents control all data." |
| "This sounds complicated to implement." | "We handle onboarding. You just introduce Tizzy to clients; we do training, setup, and ongoing support." |
| "How do I know this actually works?" | "We're collecting outcome data in our pilot (ASHA benchmarks: topic maintenance, repair, reciprocity). Happy to share early results." |
| "I don't have time to learn another tool." | "Dashboard is optional. Many SLPs just refer clients and receive updates via monthly reports. Zero ongoing time commitment required." |

---

#### Step 4: Pilot Launch (Week 7-12)

**Pilot Structure:**
- Enroll 3-5 clients from clinic
- Provide free or subsidized devices ($0-100 per client)
- 90-day pilot period
- Weekly check-ins with therapist
- Pre/post assessments (therapist-administered)

**Success Metrics:**
- Client retention: 70%+ complete 90 days
- Measurable improvement: 60%+ improve on 1+ ASHA benchmark
- Therapist satisfaction: 4.5/5 rating (dashboard, insights, outcomes)
- Parent satisfaction: 4/5 rating (product usability, child progress)

**Pilot Output:**
- Case study (with clinic consent): "[Clinic Name] improves autism therapy outcomes with Tizzy"
- Testimonial (video or written): "Tizzy helps me see what's happening outside my office."
- Data for publication (if research collaboration model)

---

#### Step 5: Convert to Paid Partnership (Month 4-6)

**Transition Plan:**
- After 90-day pilot, present results (improvement data, user feedback)
- Propose paid partnership (referral, license, or hybrid)
- Lock in annual commitment (discounted Year 1 pricing)
- Launch co-marketing (clinic website, social media, email newsletter)

**Conversion Rate Target:** 60-70% of pilots convert to paid partnerships

## Partnership Pillar 2: Schools & Universities

### Why Schools Matter (And Why They're Hard)

**Opportunity:**
- 500,000+ autistic students in K-12 (U.S.)
- School-based SLPs are largest employer of SLPs (55,000+ positions)
- Transitions are high-pain moments (middle school, high school, college)

**Challenges:**
- FERPA compliance (vendor agreements, data handling)
- Long procurement cycles (6-12 months, RFPs, board approvals)
- Budget constraints (special ed funding, grant-dependent)
- Policy resistance (recording in schools = sensitive)

**Strategic Approach:** Schools are a Year 2-3 priority, not Year 1. Prove value with therapy clinics first.

---

### Target School Profiles

**Tier 1 (Highest ROI):**
- **College Disability Services Offices:** Transition support for autistic students (our pilot wedge)
- **Private schools with autism programs:** Faster decision-making, less bureaucracy
- **Charter schools (autism-focused):** Mission-aligned, innovation-friendly

**Tier 2 (Institutional Scale):**
- **Large urban school districts:** 5,000-50,000 students, dedicated special ed departments
- **Autism-specialized schools:** The Monarch School, Spectrum Academy, etc.
- **University autism support programs:** College Autism Network members

**Tier 3 (Long-Term):**
- **State-wide special ed programs:** Policy-level adoption
- **Federal partnerships:** Dept of Education pilots, IEP innovation grants

---

### Partnership Value Proposition for Schools

**For School Administrators:**
- "Support autistic students during critical transitions (middle school, high school, college)."
- "Reduce social conflicts, improve peer relationships, lower dropout/transfer rates."
- "Evidence-based assistive technology that complies with FERPA and student privacy laws."

**For School-Based SLPs:**
- "Extend your impact beyond pull-out sessions—see what happens in classrooms, cafeteria, recess."
- "Data-driven IEP goals: measure real-world progress on social communication benchmarks."
- "Reduce caseload pressure: Tizzy provides ongoing coaching between sessions."

**For Parents (via School):**
- "Your school offers Tizzy as an accommodation/assistive technology tool."
- "See how your child navigates school conversations—support them at home."
- "Free or subsidized through school district program."

---

### Partnership Models for Schools

#### Model A: Pilot Program (Grant-Funded)

**Structure:**
- School applies for grant (special ed innovation, autism support, assistive tech)
- Tizzy provides devices at cost or subsidized ($100-150 per student)
- 50-100 students, 6-12 month pilot
- Pre/post outcome assessments (social-emotional IEP goals)
- Publish results (case study, district report)

**School Benefits:**
- Free or heavily subsidized devices
- Evidence-based intervention for IEPs
- Publishable outcomes (demonstrate special ed effectiveness)

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Institutional credibility (proof Tizzy works at scale)
- Dataset growth (hundreds of students, diverse contexts)
- Referenceable case study for future sales

**Best For:** Year 2 (after therapy channel validates outcomes)

---

#### Model B: District-Wide License

**Structure:**
- District purchases annual licenses ($100-150 per student per year)
- Includes devices, training, admin dashboard, compliance support
- Offered to autistic students as IEP accommodation
- Parents opt in (explicit consent, FERPA-compliant)

**School Benefits:**
- Scalable assistive technology for entire special ed population
- Measurable outcomes (social-emotional goals, peer relationships)
- Vendor handles compliance, training, support

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Predictable institutional revenue ($500K-1M per large district)
- Long-term contracts (3-5 year commitments)
- National expansion pipeline (50 districts = $25M+ TAM)

**Best For:** Year 3+ (after pilots prove outcomes)

---

#### Model C: College Transition Program

**Structure:**
- University disability services offers Tizzy to incoming autistic freshmen
- Free or subsidized devices (funded by university, grants, or parents)
- 90-day transition support (first semester)
- Weekly check-ins with disability services counselor

**School Benefits:**
- Reduce autistic student dropout rates (retention = tuition revenue)
- Differentiated support offering (recruiting advantage)
- Positive outcomes (student success, parent satisfaction)

**Tizzy Benefits:**
- Immediate wedge (college pilot is Year 1 GTM)
- High-value users (young adults, motivated, tech-savvy)
- Word-of-mouth within autism community

**Best For:** Year 1 (this is the pilot strategy)

## Partnership Pillar 3: Autism Organizations & Research Institutions

### Why Autism Organizations Matter

**Community Credibility:**
- Autism self-advocacy orgs (ASAN, Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network) = trust validators
- Parent orgs (Autism Speaks, Autism Society) = distribution and referrals
- Research institutions (NIH, Simons Foundation) = clinical validation and funding

**Co-Design & Governance:**
- Autistic advisors ensure product respects neurodiversity
- Avoid "fix autism" narratives (focus on coaching, not cure)
- Community endorsement = differentiation from "creepy tech"

---

### Target Organizations

**Tier 1 (Autism Self-Advocacy):**
- ASAN (Autistic Self Advocacy Network)
- Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network (AWN)
- Autistic People of Color Fund

**Tier 2 (Parent & Family Orgs):**
- Autism Society (local chapters)
- Autism Speaks (controversial, but large reach)
- National Autism Association

**Tier 3 (Research & Clinical):**
- Simons Foundation (SFARI research grants)
- NIH NIMH (autism research funding)
- Marcus Autism Center (Emory University)
- MIND Institute (UC Davis)

---

### Partnership Models

#### Model A: Advisory Board (Community Co-Design)

**Structure:**
- 5-10 autistic advisors (paid stipends: $1,000-2,000/year)
- Quarterly meetings (product feedback, policy input, roadmap review)
- Co-design features (haptic patterns, coaching language, privacy controls)

**Benefits to Tizzy:**
- Community legitimacy ("Built with autistic input, not just for autistic people")
- Avoid harmful design decisions (masking pressure, conformity narratives)
- Early feedback prevents costly product pivots

**Benefits to Advisors:**
- Paid role, professional development
- Influence on assistive tech that impacts their community
- Platform to advocate for neurodiversity-affirming design

---

#### Model B: Research Collaboration

**Structure:**
- Partner with autism research centers (Simons, MIND Institute, etc.)
- Co-apply for NIH/NSF research grants ($500K-2M over 3 years)
- Conduct controlled studies (Tizzy vs. therapy-as-usual)
- Publish peer-reviewed research (co-authored)

**Benefits to Tizzy:**
- Gold-standard clinical validation
- Access to research participants (consented, IRB-approved)
- Publications = credibility for institutional sales

**Benefits to Researchers:**
- Novel dataset (real-world conversational data)
- Cutting-edge technology (voice AI, wearables)
- Publications, grants, academic recognition

---

#### Model C: Distribution Partnership

**Structure:**
- Autism organizations promote Tizzy to members (email, social media, events)
- Tizzy provides scholarships for low-income families (via org)
- Revenue share or affiliate program (10% commission on sales)

**Benefits to Tizzy:**
- Access to large parent/autistic communities (Autism Society = 100K+ members)
- Trust signal ("Endorsed by [Org Name]")
- Mission alignment (impact-first positioning)

**Benefits to Organizations:**
- Additional funding (revenue share supports programs)
- Member value (access to cutting-edge assistive tech)
- Positive outcomes (better tools for constituents)
