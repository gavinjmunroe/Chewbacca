# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in the Tizzy project a harassment-free, autism-inclusive experience for everyone, regardless of:

- Age
- Body size
- Visible or invisible disability
- Ethnicity
- Sex characteristics
- Gender identity and expression
- Level of experience
- Education
- Socio-economic status
- Nationality
- Personal appearance
- Race
- Caste
- Color
- Religion
- Sexual identity and orientation
- Neurotype or neurodiversity
- Communication style

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community that actively centers and values autistic voices.

## Autism-Inclusive Principles

This project exists to serve the autism community. Our Code of Conduct reflects neurodiversity-affirming values:

### 1. Centering Autistic Voices

- **"Nothing About Us Without Us":** Decisions affecting autistic users must include autistic input
- **Lived Experience Matters:** Personal experiences are valid and valuable contributions
- **Self-Advocacy:** We support autistic self-advocates and respect their expertise
- **Presumption of Competence:** We assume everyone has valuable perspectives to share

### 2. Respecting Different Communication Styles

- **Direct Communication:** Being straightforward is valued, not rude
- **Literal Language:** Say what you mean; avoid unclear hints or implications
- **Written Preference:** Text-based communication is always acceptable
- **Processing Time:** It's okay to need time to formulate responses
- **No Small Talk Required:** Get to the point; social niceties are optional
- **Clarification Welcome:** Always okay to ask "what do you mean?"

### 3. Supporting Diverse Needs

- **Sensory Considerations:** Respect that some people may avoid certain stimuli (e.g., voice chat, video calls)
- **Executive Function:** Understand that organizational challenges are valid
- **Hyperfocus & Interest:** Passionate detailed focus on topics is valued, not discouraged
- **Social Energy:** No pressure to engage beyond your capacity
- **Masking-Free Zone:** Be yourself; don't force neurotypical social performance

### 4. Accessible Communication

- **Clear & Concrete:** Use specific, unambiguous language
- **Structure:** Organize information with headers, lists, clear formatting
- **Content Warnings:** Flag potentially overwhelming content (e.g., sensory details, health topics)
- **Multiple Formats:** Provide information in various ways when possible
- **Plain Language:** Avoid unnecessary jargon; explain technical terms

### 5. Flexibility & Understanding

- **Grace for Differences:** Communication differences are not personal offenses
- **Assume Good Intent:** Interpret statements generously unless harm is clear
- **Misunderstandings Happen:** Address them calmly and move forward
- **No Tone Policing:** Focus on content, not perceived "tone"
- **Neurodiversity is Natural:** Different neurotypes approach things differently - that's good!

## Our Standards

### Examples of Behavior That Contributes to a Positive Environment

#### General Conduct
- Using welcoming, inclusive, and respectful language
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences
- Gracefully accepting constructive criticism
- Focusing on what is best for the community and end users
- Showing empathy toward other community members
- Acknowledging and learning from mistakes

#### Autism-Specific Positive Behaviors
- Respecting varied communication preferences (written vs. verbal, direct vs. contextual)
- Accommodating different processing speeds without rushing
- Using clear, literal language and avoiding ambiguous phrasing
- Providing content warnings for sensory or triggering content
- Celebrating special interests and hyperfocus as strengths
- Advocating for accessibility and inclusion
- Supporting autistic leadership and decision-making
- Challenging ableist language and assumptions
- Respecting stimming, communication devices, and adaptive tools
- Recognizing that "challenging behavior" often communicates unmet needs

### Examples of Unacceptable Behavior

#### General Misconduct
- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting
- Sustained disruption of discussions or collaboration

#### Ableist and Harmful Behaviors (Zero Tolerance)
- Using ableist language, slurs, or insults (e.g., "r-word," "crazy," "psycho," "high/low functioning")
- Promoting or advocating for Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) or other compliance-based therapies that suppress autistic traits
- Supporting organizations or rhetoric that views autism as a disease to be cured or eliminated
- Dismissing or invalidating lived experiences of autistic individuals
- Speaking over autistic people about autism-related topics
- Tone policing or demanding "appropriate" emotional expression
- Criticizing stimming, echolalia, or other autistic traits
- Forcing unwanted social contact (e.g., eye contact, hugs, voice communication)
- Mocking or belittling special interests or communication differences
- Pathologizing autistic traits or framing them as deficits
- Promoting harmful "treatments" (e.g., MMS/bleach, chelation, restrictive diets without medical basis)
- Gaslighting about sensory experiences or support needs
- Using functioning labels to dismiss someone's perspective
- Weaponizing diagnosis (or lack thereof) to invalidate contributions

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

### Enforcement Priorities

When enforcing this Code of Conduct, we prioritize:
1. **Safety of marginalized community members**, especially autistic individuals
2. **Protection from ableism and discrimination**
3. **Maintaining an accessible and inclusive environment**
4. **Preventing harm over protecting reputation**

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples include:

- Using an official project e-mail address
- Posting via an official social media account
- Acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event
- Participating in project GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, and discussions

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these guidelines in determining consequences for violations:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact:** Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence:** A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact:** A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence:** A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact:** A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence:** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact:** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence:** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

### Special Considerations for Ableism

- **First-time unintentional ableist language:** Education and correction (e.g., explaining why "high/low functioning" labels are harmful)
- **Repeated or intentional ableism:** Immediate temporary ban
- **Promotion of harmful therapies or hate rhetoric:** Immediate permanent ban
- **Harassment of autistic community members:** Immediate permanent ban
