# Reinventing Chewbacca, from the inside

Written 2026-10-13 from Caleb's answers that night. Evolve the repo we have,
keep it local, keep the team building off each other, keep the name. Every
phase names the number that says it worked, and nothing counts as done until
the person it's for has used it.

## What Caleb decided

- First person besides Caleb to use it: Ryan Korn (Zeutara).
- Ships first: the growth engineer for clients. "Once we've got it rlly good at
  doing that that'll mean it's capable of doing pretty much anything."
- Ryan does all of it eventually, starting with GTME. Where it runs for him
  waits on Ryan and Sagar talking.
- Keep evolving this repo. No clean restart.
- Don't split Caleb's setup from the product yet; the team builds off each other.
- Local for now. Businesses feel safer with it on their own machine, and the
  GTM tools are easier to drive from a signed-in Mac. Email data may move to
  treg or other providers.
- Chewbacca is Caleb and Gavin. Split not decided.
- The name stays Chewbacca for now.
- Money gets found when it's needed. Concurrency sizes itself; Caleb often runs
  8+ tabs.
- Public launch: decided later, after B2B.

## The north star

Ryan runs Zeutara's outbound from Chewbacca on his own Mac, and meetings get
booked, without Caleb in the loop. The scoreboard is booked meetings and real
positive replies (read from reply text, never a category), weekly, from the API.

## Phase 0. Know what's used (Cole)

Caleb's answer to "what do you use" was "I would hope everything". The 10/13
boundary audit found 44 of 138 skills unloaded in 30 days. Measure instead of
hoping: one weekly report from the verdict log and skill routing of what fired,
what was loaded, what refused, and what nobody touched.
Number: every skill, command and hook has a 30-day use count.

## Phase 1. Ryan's Mac, zero to first graded draft

The stranger install is Chewbacca's weakest path and Ryan is the stranger.
From a clean Mac with repo access: install, connect his GTM accounts in his own
signed-in browser, load Zeutara's client config, and produce a graded draft.
Nothing on his Mac needs Caleb's brain, phone numbers or personal files.
Number: under 30 minutes, every prompt and error counted, rehearsed on a scratch
home first, then done once with Ryan.

## Phase 2. One engine, pluggable providers (Daniel)

Continue the strangler (REPORT-one-engine, moves 0 to 5 landed or in flight,
golden check at 0 diffs). Then providers behind one interface each: lists and
enrichment (Clay, treg), verification (treg first, it wins on own-mailbox
sends), sending mailboxes (whatever the client uses). A client is a config file.
Number: a second client from config to first graded draft in under a day; a
provider swap changes config only.

## Phase 3. The operator's front door

Ryan and Jonah see one decision inbox: only the calls that need them, each with
a take, everything else as a one-line receipt (built 10/13, unseen live). The
Slack reply flow Sagar asks about daily is the same feed.
Number: decisions per day per operator, and hours Caleb spends watching.

## Phase 4. Fast and quiet

Hooks cost about 1.3 s of CPU a command, 94% of it the write logger. Flip the
Rust write-log once 1,000 fresh rows show only same-second mismatches. Move the
launch guard's shell half into `chewbacca clay` (Caleb and Sagar's call; 103
refusals since 10/12 3pm, about 1 real). Nothing an agent does ever draws on the
screen or takes focus; a test that does fails.
Number: under 0.3 s of hook CPU a command; zero windows, toasts or dialogs from
agents in a week.

## Phase 5. Everything else keeps moving behind it

The Mac OS layer, the USC stack, the browser extension and texting Chewbacca
keep going in the background, ranked by hours saved, never ahead of Phase 1 to 4.

## What would prove this plan wrong

If Ryan can't get from a clean Mac to a graded draft without Caleb on a call,
local-first is the wrong bet for clients and the server path comes back.
