**The number to use, checked 2026-10-13.** Menlo Ventures, "2026: The State of Consumer AI" (Sept 15, 2026; 5,067 US adults with Morning Consult, July 2026): 64% of US adults have used AI, and 55% of AI users pay for at least one AI product, which Menlo puts at roughly 90 million Americans, so about 1 in 3 US adults. The "only 2% pay" line Caleb used on 10/13 is Menlo's 2025 estimate that about 3% of the world's 1.8 billion AI users paid. That was global and a model estimate; the 55% is a US survey. Menlo's own footnote compares the two, so the jump overstates the real change.

**Why the rest don't use it.** Pew Research Center, "Why don't people use chatbots?" (June 11, 2026; 5,119 US adults, Feb 17 to 23, 2026): about half of US adults never use a chatbot. Among them: not interested 83% (60% a major reason), privacy 79% (54%), don't trust accuracy 76% (45%), don't know how 55% (29%), fear of being judged 14% (3%). 67% say they are unlikely to start in the next year. Menlo's holdouts: 70% distrust what AI says, 76% cite privacy, 83% would rather deal with a person.

**Who pays.** Menlo: reliance predicts paying better than income. Payers use AI daily at 50% vs 26% for non-payers, and 65% of payers use an agent vs 13%. The typical payer spends $20 to $49 a month. Menlo's advice to founders is to keep a free tier, because that is where the habit forms.

**How to apply:** quote the 55% / 1 in 3 figure with Menlo named, never "2%". For the people who don't pay, lead with a job they already do every day (texts, calendar, email), say plainly what stays on the Mac (`chewbacca trust`), and never open on a blank box.
