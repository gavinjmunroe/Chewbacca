# Domain corpora: making Chewbacca a master at a craft

Caleb, 2026-10-10: "Make a list of every possible list we could make... think
abt literally every digital service ever... every role... everything a student
could ever do." The master list of about 200 domains is in his Downloads as
`Chewb mastery lists.md`; AI, dev and context engineering goes first.

The template is the dev creators corpus from 2026-10-09
(`~/second-brain/raw/creators/`): scouts build a graph of practitioners, an
ingest pulls their material, and a distill pass writes a ranked "adopt into
Chewbacca" list, with a source for every item.

## What a domain list pulls

- Official docs, shortcuts and changelog for the tool
- Practitioners: YouTube, X, IG, TikTok, blogs, newsletters, podcasts
- Their repos, templates, presets and plugins
- Teardowns by people who do it for a living
- Certification exam guides (Adobe, Google, Microsoft, AWS): a free syllabus
  of every skill a pro gets tested on
- Forums and subreddits for failure modes the docs skip
- Real examples labeled good and bad, so the kit can judge work, not just make it

## Never-dos, each one already paid for

- Never cut the scope he set. On 2026-10-09 the creator ingest was cut to 36
  of 238 with three platforms on hold, and he said "You didn't listen to me."
  A real limit is a `BLOCKED:` line, and the rest keeps going.
- Never report a corpus as ingested when part of it is missing. Count the
  empties: on 10/10, 63 creators had never been pulled and 25 more had
  empty folders, so the status had to say so.
- Never rank practitioners by how many scouts found them. Only 14 of 238 were
  found more than once, and those were the famous ones, not the best fit.
- Never call a domain mastered because its corpus was read. It counts once
  the kit beats bare Claude on 20 held-out tasks in that domain, sealed before
  the ingest. An adopt list with nothing built is just reading.
