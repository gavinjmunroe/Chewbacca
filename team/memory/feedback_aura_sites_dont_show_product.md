Caleb, 2026-10-07, stopping a phone-and-feature-cards build for the Amber fridge site: "Bro don't show the product. Eigen aura farmed by not showing the product."

**Why:** teameigen.com never explains Eigen. Its room shows the team's polaroids, a typed founder letter, the books they read, a video of someone working, and one button. The pull comes from the world and the people, and the mystery makes you want in. A page of feature cards reads as a pitch deck, the opposite of aura.

**How to apply:** on any brand or waitlist site, each object opens onto something real about the people and what they believe (a letter, a photo, a story, a voice), never onto a product screen, a capability demo or a mock UI. This supersedes the "each element proves a capability" line in the interface skill for aura sites. Related: [[project_amber_fridge_site]], [[feedback_fridge_night_2026_10_07]].

**Same morning, a minute later:** "Don't show actual ppl that loses aura." No team photos, no named real people either. The objects themselves carry it: a household's things, their backs, their handwriting, never a face or a bio.

**Later the same morning:** "I don't like the background scaling up and down, I want the objects to come off the fridge into the foreground. Also the text around the notecard is so little aura." The room never zooms. Objects travel out of their real spot toward the viewer, and no instructions, titles or labelled buttons surround them. Every affordance lives in the world: a blank line, a pen, a page you can close.

**The pattern behind four corrections in one hour (clock, pen, ribbon, leather):** every physical thing drawn in CSS or SVG on top of the 3D render read as fake next to rendered objects. On a rendered scene, every physical object is rendered in the scene (or rendered as its own registered layer), and the DOM only adds hit areas, motion and the user's own ink. A missing object beats a drawn one.
