# Core Features: Job-to-Be-Done and Coaching Taxonomy

## The Job-to-Be-Done

### Single Sentence JTBD (Clean, Non-Fuzzy)

**Tizzy helps a person improve real-world conversational outcomes by detecting the specific conversational behaviors that are holding them back, then coaching those behaviors in the moment and across time.**

---

## The Measurable Promise

### The 30-Day Proof of Value

Within 30 days, Tizzy:
1. **Identifies** the user's highest-impact conversational patterns
2. **Shows them** where these patterns occur
3. **Provides** a coaching plan that measurably improves at least 1-2 target skills

**Examples of target skills:**
- Fewer conversational breakdowns
- Better topic maintenance
- Better repair after missteps
- Fewer missed social cues

---

### North Star Outcomes (Longer Horizon)

The ultimate outcomes Tizzy drives toward:

- **Better friendships** - more satisfying, stable relationships
- **Fewer misunderstandings** - reduced social friction
- **Fewer social "crashes"** - avoided escalations and meltdowns
- **More confidence** - reduced social anxiety
- **Less masking burnout** - authentic communication without exhaustion

**Critical principle:** You must not accidentally teach "mask harder." You teach "communicate clearly while honoring yourself."

## The Autism Coaching Taxonomy (V1)

### Why This Matters

Autism is defined partly by persistent social communication and interaction differences, including back-and-forth conversation challenges and social reciprocity differences (CDC).

**Your V1 coaching taxonomy must map to real speech-language and autism frameworks, otherwise you will build vibes instead of outcomes.**

---

### Bucket A: Reciprocity and Turn-Taking

**The "back-and-forth" problem**

#### What It Includes
- Interruptions (cutting others off)
- Monologuing (talking too long without pausing)
- Delayed responses (taking too long to respond)
- Not asking return questions (one-sided conversation)
- Failing to respond to conversational bids

#### Clinical Foundation
DSM-5 explicitly calls out "failure of normal back-and-forth conversation" as an example of social-emotional reciprocity deficits (Autism Speaks).

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Double pulse when you've been talking for 90+ seconds without pause
- **Post-convo insight:** "You talked for 3 minutes straight about Pokémon. Try pausing to ask Emma a question after 30-60 seconds."
- **Trend data:** "You're interrupting 40% less than 2 weeks ago - great progress!"

---

### Bucket B: Topic Management

**Start, maintain, shift, and end topics appropriately**

#### What It Includes
- Topic initiation (starting conversations appropriately)
- Topic maintenance (staying on topic)
- Topic shifting (smooth transitions)
- Topic ending (graceful conclusions)
- Repair after topic derailment

#### Clinical Foundation
Speech-language benchmarks explicitly include topic maintenance, repair, and increased number of turns (ASHA).

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Single pulse when topic has drifted for 2+ turns
- **Post-convo insight:** "The conversation shifted from lunch to video games. You didn't notice and kept talking about lunch. Try listening for topic changes."
- **Trend data:** "You maintained topic for average 4.2 turns this week, up from 2.8 last week."

---

### Bucket C: Repair and Recovery

**What happens after a misstep**

#### What It Includes
- Noticing confusion in the conversation
- Clarifying intent when misunderstood
- Apologizing appropriately
- Rephrasing when not understood
- "Backing out" gracefully from awkward moments

#### Why This Is High ROI
**It prevents one mistake from becoming a full social failure.** Repair is often more important than avoiding all mistakes.

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Long pulse when partner shows confusion markers (long pause, "what?", tone shift)
- **Post-convo insight:** "When Jordan said 'I don't follow,' you changed the subject instead of clarifying. Try: 'Let me explain that better.'"
- **Trend data:** "You attempted repair 6 times this week (up from 1 last week). Keep practicing!"

---

### Bucket D: Prosody, Pacing, Intensity, and Timing

**How you sound, not just what you say**

#### What It Includes
- Tone mismatch (too flat, too intense, doesn't match context)
- Speaking too fast or too slow
- Volume inappropriate for context
- Unusual rhythm or stress patterns
- Timing of pauses

#### Why Acoustic Models Matter Here
**This is exactly where acoustic models matter more than transcripts.** You cannot detect tone mismatch from words alone.

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Single pulse when speaking 30% faster than baseline
- **Post-convo insight:** "Your intensity was very high while talking about the movie (excitement). Sarah seemed overwhelmed. Try matching her energy level."
- **Trend data:** "Your pace has stabilized to 140-160 words/min (down from 190). Much better!"

---

### Bucket E: Nonverbal Mismatch Proxy Signals

**Without cameras in V1, infer breakdown moments**

#### What It Includes (Audio-Only Proxies)
- Long pauses (confusion or discomfort likely)
- Overlapping speech (interruption or excitement)
- Abrupt topic shifts (disengagement signal)
- Sudden silence (breakdown or processing)
- Escalation markers (volume, pitch, intensity increasing)

#### Limitation Acknowledgment
You cannot read facial expressions without video, but you **can** infer breakdown moments from audio patterns.

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Double pulse when 5+ second pause after your statement (likely confusion)
- **Post-convo insight:** "After you explained your project, there was a 7-second pause. That often means the listener is confused or doesn't know how to respond."
- **Trend data:** "Awkward pauses decreased 30% this week - your clarity is improving."

---

### Bucket F: Theory of Mind Markers and Implied Meaning

**Understanding what's not said directly**

#### What It Includes
- Literal interpretation moments (missing implied meaning)
- Missed implied requests ("I'm cold" meaning "close the window")
- Sarcasm detection failures
- Not recognizing indirect communication
- Assuming others share your knowledge

#### Clinical Foundation
ASHA's social communication benchmarks include theory of mind concepts and recognizing sarcasm (ASHA).

#### Important Caveat
Tizzy should not "diagnose," but it **can** coach patterns like literal interpretation moments and missed implied requests.

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** (Limited - too complex for real-time)
- **Post-convo insight:** "When Mom said 'It's freezing in here,' she might have wanted you to close the window, not just agree it's cold."
- **Trend data:** "You're asking 'What do you mean by that?' more often - great clarification strategy!"

---

### Bucket G: Social Calibration

**Politeness, boundaries, and context fit**

#### What It Includes
- Oversharing (too much personal information too soon)
- Under-sharing (not reciprocating disclosure)
- Blunt phrasing (technically true but socially harsh)
- Context-inappropriate jokes or topics
- Boundary violations (physical or conversational)

#### Critical Design Principle
**This is sensitive: coaching must be user-aligned, not conformity-driven.**

You are NOT teaching:
- ❌ "Never talk about your interests"
- ❌ "Always be indirect"
- ❌ "Suppress your honesty"

You ARE teaching:
- ✅ "Notice when others seem uncomfortable"
- ✅ "Different contexts have different norms"
- ✅ "Honesty + kindness is possible"

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** (Limited - requires nuance)
- **Post-convo insight:** "You shared details about your digestive issues within 2 minutes of meeting Alex. That's a lot of personal info for a first conversation. Consider waiting until you know someone better."
- **Trend data:** "You're adjusting your topics based on context more often (work vs. friends)."

---

### Bucket H: Sensory Overload and Conversational Collapse

**When the system is overwhelmed**

#### What It Includes
- Acoustic stress cues (voice strain, shakiness)
- Conversation breakdown risk (multiple overlapping stressors)
- Escalation toward meltdown/shutdown
- Exit and reset strategies
- Recovery after overload

#### Why This Is First-Class in Tizzy
**Overload is not a "failure state" - it's a real neurodivergent experience that needs support.**

Tizzy should:
- Detect early signs
- Coach exits and resets
- Normalize taking breaks
- Track overload patterns over time

#### Tizzy Coaching Examples
- **In-moment haptic:** Long pulse when stress markers detected + conversation complexity high ("Exit soon if you need to")
- **Post-convo insight:** "Your voice showed stress after 12 minutes of the party. That's your typical limit in loud environments. Plan breaks for next time."
- **Trend data:** "You successfully exited and reset 4 times this week before full overload. Huge progress!"

## Haptic Coaching Language (V1)

### Start with 3 Patterns and Expand

| Pattern | Meaning | Coaching Bucket(s) | Example Trigger |
|---------|---------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Single pulse** | Pacing or volume adjustment | D (Prosody) | Speaking 30%+ faster than baseline |
| **Double pulse** | Cue to ask a return question or clarify | A (Reciprocity), B (Topic) | Talked for 90+ seconds without pause |
| **Long pulse** | Escalation risk or overload risk, cue to exit/reset | H (Overload), E (Breakdown) | Stress markers + high conversation complexity |

### Design Principles for Haptics

- **Subtle** - not alarming or embarrassing
- **Learnable** - consistent meanings
- **Useful** - actionable in the moment
- **Optional** - user can disable specific patterns

## Post-Conversation Review Format

### What the User Sees After a Conversation

The app shows a **timeline of moments that matter**, not full surveillance playback.

#### Review Components

1. **Quick Summary (15 seconds to read)**
   - Duration: 8 minutes
   - People: You + 2 others
   - Context: Lunch at school
   - Overall: Mostly positive, 1 challenge

2. **What Went Well (Top 2)**
   - ✅ You asked 3 return questions - great reciprocity!
   - ✅ Good repair when Jordan seemed confused

3. **What to Try Next Time (Top 2)**
   - 💡 You talked about video games for 4 minutes while others looked bored. Try reading the room.
   - 💡 When you got excited, your volume jumped 40%. Try staying at conversation level.

4. **One Micro-Goal for Tomorrow**
   - "When you've been talking for 1 minute, pause and ask the other person a question."

5. **Optional: Annotated Clips**
   - Only if explicitly enabled
   - Short (10-30 second) clips of specific moments
   - With annotation overlay explaining what happened

6. **Optional: Practice Drill**
   - Short roleplay suggestion
   - "Practice: Have a 2-minute conversation where you ask 2 questions about the other person's weekend."

## Optional Therapist View

### With Explicit Opt-In

This is a **huge wedge for therapy effectiveness**, but also a governance minefield.

#### What Therapists Can See (If User Opts In)

1. **Weekly summary report**
   - Progress on IEP goals or therapy targets
   - Most frequent patterns
   - Trend data

2. **Selected conversation insights**
   - User or parent can choose which conversations to share
   - Insights only (not raw audio) by default
   - Raw clips only if explicitly enabled

3. **Goal-setting interface**
   - Therapist can set coaching goals
   - Tizzy tracks progress toward those goals
   - Collaborative planning

#### Privacy Protections

- **Explicit opt-in required** (not default)
- **User can revoke at any time**
- **Audit log** of what therapist accessed
- **User controls** which conversations are shared
- **HIPAA-aware** design (even if not HIPAA-covered in V1)
