# An intro montage is never a slideshow

From the Firm Foundation hero intro, 2026-10-18. Three intros were rejected in one hour before one held.

## What failed, in his words

- Fourteen stills graded to one duotone, cut every 3 frames behind the title: "the background changing is kinda low aura it's not creative nowhere near as goated as the reel".
- Four full-colour stills, each with a camera move (push, spin, track, rise) and an iris transition: "motion for the sake of motion and adds nothing", "It CANT read like a slideshow with only simple background changes!!!!!!!", "If I wanted to send them a slideshow I'd send them a slideshow".
- A starfield that filled a flat box: "the dots being a firm rectangle is so low aura it's supposed to feel like a 3d universe".
- Extra guide marks (rulers, captions, flickering pixels) added to look like the reference: "No need to spam things that don't add anything", "Everything should be intentional".
- Picking a tech image by a private rationale (a 1950s radio mic for "technology"): "Wait why a microphone lmao???". Pick what the viewer reads at a glance as the thing the VO says, for this audience (a church site on a phone).

## What held

- One material carries the whole intro and becomes each picture. In ff-hero-video that's `src/v7/Dust.tsx`: one set of grains bursts into a 3D cloud (perspective, dolly, yaw), condenses into each image on its VO word, leaves through depth and condenses into the next, then becomes the ending figure. The pictures are made by the transition, never swapped behind it.
- Fewer pictures, each held long enough to read. Two plus the ending beat four in the same 1.5 s.
- Every mark has one job. The red guides became a crosshair, one circle and two fingertip boxes.

## Before building an intro

1. Write what persists across every cut. If the answer is "the title", the background is a slideshow.
2. Name the one move each picture makes and what it means. If you can't say what it means, cut it.
3. Show a frame grid of the motion (6 to 10 fps), not stills, before calling it done.
