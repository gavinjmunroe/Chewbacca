## AI and Voice Technology

### Modulate AI - Ensemble Listening Model (ELM)

**Source:** Modulate Press Release
**URL:** https://www.modulate.ai/press-releases/modulate-unveils-the-ensemble-listening-model-elm-a-fundamentally-new-ai-architecture-optimized-for-accuracy-transparency-and-cost
**Date:** 2024-2025
**Relevance:** Core voice understanding technology for Tizzy

**Key Points:**
- ELM is positioned as voice-first understanding that combines spoken words with acoustic signals (emotion, prosody)
- Claims superior accuracy for voice intent understanding compared to general foundation models
- More cost-effective than general AI models
- Optimized for transparency and accuracy

**Strategic Importance:**
- This technology enables Tizzy to go beyond transcript-only analysis
- Captures "how it was said" not just "what was said"
- Critical for detecting prosody, timing, tone, emotional volatility
- Potential vendor partnership/licensing relationship

**Critical Considerations:**
- Modulate cannot be Tizzy's foundation (vendor lock-in risk)
- Tizzy's moat must be autism dataset + coaching design, not API access
- Need clear IP boundaries in any partnership agreement

## Autism: Clinical Definitions and Diagnostic Criteria

### CDC - Autism Signs and Symptoms

**Source:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
**URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/autism/signs-symptoms/index.html
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Official medical definition of autism spectrum disorder

**Key Points:**
- Autism defined by persistent differences in social communication and interaction
- Includes back-and-forth conversation challenges
- Social reciprocity differences are core diagnostic features
- Patterns must be present from early development

**Application to Tizzy:**
- Validates conversation coaching as addressing core autism features
- Back-and-forth conversation is explicitly named as challenge area
- Coaching taxonomy should map to DSM-5 diagnostic criteria
- Positions Tizzy as addressing medically recognized needs

### Autism Speaks - DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria

**Source:** Autism Speaks
**URL:** https://www.autismspeaks.org/autism-diagnostic-criteria-dsm-5
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Detailed diagnostic criteria including conversation deficits

**Key Points:**
- DSM-5 explicitly calls out "failure of normal back-and-forth conversation"
- Social-emotional reciprocity deficits are diagnostic feature
- Includes difficulty adjusting behavior to social contexts
- Persistent across multiple contexts and settings

**Application to Tizzy:**
- "Back-and-forth conversation" is perfect framing for reciprocity coaching
- Turn-taking, interruptions, monologuing are all relevant
- Multiple context requirement validates always-wearable approach
- Coaching should help users recognize and adjust to contexts

**V1 Coaching Implications:**
- Reciprocity coaching is evidence-based
- Topic management and repair are diagnostically relevant
- Context awareness is clinically important

## Speech-Language Pathology Frameworks

### ASHA - Social Communication Benchmarks

**Source:** American Speech-Language-Hearing Association
**URL:** https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/social-communication-disorder/social-communication-benchmarks/
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Clinical framework for measurable communication goals

**Key Points:**
- Benchmarks include topic maintenance, repair, turn-taking
- Increased number of conversational turns is measurable outcome
- Theory of mind concepts included (sarcasm recognition, implied meaning)
- Age-appropriate benchmarks provide developmental context

**Application to Tizzy:**
- V1 coaching taxonomy should map to ASHA benchmarks
- Topic maintenance is explicitly named (coachable with haptics)
- Repair and recovery skills are clinically validated
- Provides framework for measurable 30-day outcomes

**Measurable Metrics for Tizzy:**
- Average turns per conversation (topic maintenance)
- Frequency of repair attempts
- Successful topic shifts vs. abrupt changes
- Reciprocity (questions asked vs. monologue time)

## Wearable Technology: Market Examples and Lessons

### Friend Pendant - Cautionary Tale

**Source:** WIRED Review
**URL:** https://www.wired.com/story/i-hate-my-ai-friend/
**Date:** 2024-2025
**Relevance:** Real-world reaction to always-listening wearable

**Key Points:**
- Always-listening AI companion device
- Triggered "are you wearing a wire?" reactions from bystanders
- Made people socially uncomfortable
- Perceived as surveillance rather than helpful tool
- Bystander suspicion even in tech-savvy circles

**Lessons for Tizzy:**
- Social acceptance is the failure mode, not technology
- "Hidden" devices create distrust
- Positioning matters: surveillance vs. assistive tech
- Bystander experience must be considered in design
- Cannot assume "it's for me, so it's fine"

**Design Implications:**
- Tizzy must be socially legible as assistive technology
- Consent mechanism is non-negotiable
- Visible governance and user control
- Cannot be perceived as "creepy tech"

## Privacy, Legal, and Compliance

### COPPA - Voice Recordings Guidance

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission
**URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2017/10/ftc-provides-additional-guidance-coppa-voice-recordings
**Date:** October 2017
**Relevance:** Legal requirement for collecting children's voice data

**Key Points:**
- COPPA applies to voice recordings from children under 13
- Requires verifiable parental consent
- Online services that collect personal information covered
- FTC enforcement includes children's voice data

**Application to Tizzy:**
- Cannot serve under-13 users without COPPA compliance
- Parental consent must be verifiable, not just checkbox
- Need parent-controlled onboarding flow
- Phase rollout: adults first, then teens, then under-13

**Compliance Requirements:**
- Privacy policy specifically addressing children
- Parental consent mechanism (email, credit card, etc.)
- Parental access to child's data
- Deletion capabilities
- No marketing to children

### State Recording Laws - Variation by Jurisdiction

**Source:** Justia Legal Guide
**URL:** https://www.justia.com/50-state-surveys/recording-phone-calls-and-conversations/
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Legal landscape for recording conversations

**Key Points:**
- States vary: one-party consent vs. all-party consent
- California, Florida, Massachusetts, others require all-party consent
- Federal law allows one-party consent
- Criminal and civil penalties for violations
- User must comply with most restrictive jurisdiction

**Application to Tizzy:**
- Cannot assume one-party consent is sufficient nationwide
- Design for most restrictive standard (all-party) OR restrict availability by state
- Terms of service must clarify user's legal obligations
- Travel across state lines complicates compliance

**Strategic Decision Needed:**
- Design for all-party consent (safest, most friction)
- Restrict to one-party states (limits market)
- Geo-based compliance (complex, error-prone)

### FERPA - School Vendor Requirements

**Source:** U.S. Department of Education - Protecting Student Privacy
**URL:** https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/Vendor%20FAQ.pdf
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Requirements for technology vendors working with schools

**Key Points:**
- Schools must have "direct control" over vendors accessing education records
- Written agreements required
- Vendors must follow school's data destruction timelines
- Strong data protection practices required
- School remains responsible even when vendor involved

**Application to Tizzy:**
- School partnerships require legal agreements, not just sales
- Cannot be "parent buys, kid uses at school" without school approval
- Data governance must meet school standards
- Destruction timelines may conflict with research needs

**Phase Strategy:**
- V1: Adult users (no FERPA)
- Phase 2-3: School partnerships with proper legal framework
- Not: Direct-to-consumer for school use

### FTC Health Breach Notification Rule

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission
**URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0
**Date:** Current (accessed 2026)
**Relevance:** Applies to consumer health apps even if not HIPAA-covered

**Key Points:**
- Covers vendors of personal health records not covered by HIPAA
- Connected device data can trigger this rule
- Breach notification requirements
- Even non-HIPAA apps have obligations

**Application to Tizzy:**
- Even though we're not clinical device, we handle sensitive data
- Autism diagnosis, communication patterns, voice data are sensitive
- Need breach notification plan
- Security is legal requirement, not just good practice

**Compliance Requirements:**
- Identify what constitutes "breach"
- Notification procedures (who, when, how)
- Forensic capability to detect breaches
- Preventive security measures
