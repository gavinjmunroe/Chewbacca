## 1. CONTRADICTIONS

**Grid base: 4 versus 8, and both authors break their own.**
Mizko (Al1xsdol4Pk, 0:26): *"whenever you are creating space between any element, it should be divisible by four"*. Jesse Showalter (cf95Z7Ngg8k, 0:16): *"I prefer an eight pixel grid system because it gives me a small base number"*. Jesse then hands you two values that satisfy neither grid: 6px minimum between elements (1:44, *"there should probably be at least six pixels of spacing"*) and a wrapper range of 24 to 30 (4:51, *"somewhere between 24 to 30 pixels of space"*). 30 is not divisible by 4 or 8; 6 is not divisible by 4 or 8. His final build uses 6px between elements (8:37) while claiming the 8px grid throughout. Mizko's own exception is at 6:24, where a sidebar goes 20 padding then 40 between blocks, inverting his "inner must be smaller than outer" law, justified by relatedness.

**Dark mode: invert versus never invert.** Head-on.
Sajid (9-oefwZ6Z74, 11:30): *"subtract the L value from 100 and that's all you need to do with these colors too"*. Kole Jain (EOcY3hPMQkk, 4:49): *"dark mode is not just the inverse of light mode"*, and at 5:22 *"So no, you shouldn't just inverse your colors from light to dark"*. Kole gives the mechanism Sajid's arithmetic misses (5:07): *"dark colors need to be more different than light colors to see the difference"*.

**Red: the CTA colour versus the destructive-only colour.**
Satori (HKtTNOgfhAU, 7:00): *"The red button outperformed the green button by 21% in terms of click through rate."* Kole (EOcY3hPMQkk, 5:39): *"that draws attention to the button and doesn't illustrate its destructive nature. Instead, we use red."* One treats red as the highest-converting primary action, the other reserves it so hard that a non-destructive red button becomes a usability bug.

**Micro-interaction duration: 150ms versus 800ms.**
Linear's CTO (wjk0ulMAkbc, 12:58): *"It has to be this instantaneous highlight, and then over 150 milliseconds fade out, because that adds a bit of quality"*. Aashish (l0EkLsZiWUg, 4:45): *"change the duration to somewhere around 800 milliseconds"* for a hamburger-to-close morph, and 600ms for a positional move (7:36). More than 5x apart for the same class of element-level state change.

**Can taste be written down?**
1NedYTQevgg (5:08, 10:34) asserts it can: brand taste lives in a markdown file, and feedback becomes *"Limit the layout to three hex codes."* Artman (wjk0ulMAkbc, 19:53) asserts the opposite about the thing consuming that file: *"No, they don't feel. ## 2. HOW TASTE IS ACQUIRED

Concrete mechanisms only, as stated.

**Linear (wjk0ulMAkbc), the densest source:**

- **The defect is not allowed to count.** 19:15: *"No, that does not count. That's a defect. You have to find a quality fix."* This is the mechanism. Assigned bugs are free; the engineer must go hunting.
- **The side effect is the point.** 15:43: *"if you think about quality all the time, and if you are aware of quality things, then you're bound to make less mistakes."* Knowing a Wednesday is coming makes engineers notice regressions in unrelated work.

## 3. SURPRISES, AND WHAT WAS WORTHLESS

**Surprises:**

- **Red beat green on a CTA by 21%** (HKtTNOgfhAU, 7:00), against the universal green-means-go convention, and against Kole's rule that red is reserved for destruction.
- **Sajid's type scale is not a scale.** Base ±2px, three sizes total, one size for an entire product surface (8:38). Conventional advice is a modular scale of six to nine steps. He also names 14px as an acceptable body size (8:38), below the usual 16px accessibility floor, while separately insisting on rem for accessibility (9:22).
- **Line height replaces bottom margin entirely** (9:43). Most spacing systems treat the two as independent tokens; he treats a manually added text margin as a bug.
- **Quality is invisible to measurement, so stop trying to measure it** (wjk0ulMAkbc, 11:21). The advice is to invest without instrumentation, which inverts the standard product-analytics posture.
- **10% of Linear's bugs are landed with no engineer involved** (5:43), stated as a shipping fact in the same talk arguing agents have no taste.
- **Fixing bugs later costs the same as fixing them now**, so the only trade is a one-time three-week feature freeze (18:18). The usual argument is compound interest; his is that the rate is constant and only the backlog is optional.
- **More output, lower average quality, measured.** Books tripled 2022 to 2025 while average quality fell (qYfTS1kDkAc, 2:23).
- **Font hinting is dead** and ignored by every modern OS except Windows (LndgfGjGImw, 17:54), which quietly invalidates a lot of inherited screen-typography lore.
- **Comic Sans may be more legible for dyslexic readers** precisely because its letterforms are inconsistent (33:16), inverting the consistency principle.
- **uxpeak uses emojis as a primary hierarchy and feedback device** (4:00, 4:42), sizing the emoji to signal selection. Most professional guidance treats emoji in UI as a smell.
- **Jesse's 6px minimum contradicts his own 8px grid** and he never notices (1:44 versus 0:16).
- **Mizko's nesting law breaks by design** (6:24): when blocks are unrelated, inner spacing legitimately exceeds outer padding. Any linter encoding "child gap < parent padding" will produce false positives on exactly this case.
