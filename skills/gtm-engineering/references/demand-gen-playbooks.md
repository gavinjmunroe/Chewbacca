# Demand-gen practitioner rules, 2025-2026

Researched 2026-10-07, with a source after every rule. Vendor benchmarks (Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist) are self-reported platform data with a sales motive. Treat them as directional.

Extended 2026-10-09 from ten practitioner and vendor skill repos, read as files and never run. Each rule below the Simpler GTM section links the exact file it came from, and each section header carries the repo's license. Where a repo has no license file, all rights are reserved by default, so those rules are paraphrased and nothing is copied. The repo-sourced numbers are the authors' own claims, mostly without a stated method.

## ColdIQ (Michel Lieben)

- Lists of 500 to 1,000 contacts with one play each. Claimed 20-30% replies against 2-3% on 100k blasts. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147, 2026-10-06; the X fetch returned 402, so these numbers come from the saved summary in second-brain `reference_lieben_cold_email_playbook`)
- Max 30 sends per inbox per day, ramped 10, then 20, then 30. Bounces under 3%. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Email 1 is plain text, no links, tracking off. He cites Hunter: +68% replies. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- An offer CTA beats a meeting ask. He cites Gong: +28% for offers, -44% for meeting asks. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- 3-step sequence, with steps 2 and 3 sent with an empty subject so they thread. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Tier accounts by lookalike-to-closed-won. (x.com/michlieben/status/2107499997906514147)
- Run LinkedIn ads at target accounts 1-2 weeks before the cold email goes out, then email the accounts that engaged. (https://kokasexton.com/insights/coldlabs.html, a secondhand summary)
- On LinkedIn: 200-300 touches a week aimed at people who engaged with a post recently, soft CTAs ("Want the link?"), voice notes capped at 25s. A third party reports 32% replies on 1,400+ messages. (https://the-linkedin-lead-machine.beehiiv.com/p/the-michel-lieben-cold-outreach-os-for-2025-26, unverified against Lieben)

## Growth Engine X (Eric Nowoslawski)

- Cold outbound fits a TAM over 100,000 and an LTV over $10,000. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- His best play is "creative ideas": AI writes 3 specific ways the product helps this prospect, drawn from preset value props and never invented freely. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- The offer has to be something competitors can't say, and so strong the prospect would pay for the call. Example: free test campaigns, or payment only after a bad review is removed, aimed at businesses rated 3.5-4.5 stars. Result: 1 positive reply per 70 emails. (https://thegtmengineer.substack.com/p/the-winning-cold-outbound-formula)
- Emails stay under 100 words. Email 1 sells the reply and never the product. Use soft CTAs. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Kill a campaign after 500-1,000 sends with no traction. Read a low open rate as spam placement and leave the subject line alone. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Over 3% bounce means stop and re-validate. Recycle a list after 3 months, 2 at minimum. Keep a warmed backup set of inboxes equal to live capacity. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)
- Run dozens of campaigns in parallel. Expect 30-60 days of warmup and testing before full stride. Email waterfall order: Leadmagic, Prospeo, GetProspect, Icypeas, TryKitt. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-eric-nowoslawski/)

## Leadbird (Nick Abraham)

- Infrastructure split: 40% Google, 40% Microsoft, 20% SMTP. Move to premium infrastructure at 40-50k emails a month. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Run two full inbox sets and swap them month on month, which gives each set a 45-day warmup cycle. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- When sender bounces pass 1-3%, the domain is used up and gets rotated. Send to hard gateways (Barracuda, Proofpoint) last, once domains have aged. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Keep it under 100 words, since fatigue sets in past 120. Personalize one variable per email and never let AI write the whole body. Strong variables: a colleague's name ("you or Liam?"), the prospect's own ICP, a competitor list. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Floor is 1% reply rate, target 2-4%. 20-40% of replies should be positive. Expect 1 qualified lead per 350-500 contacts. (https://discoveredlabs.com/podcast/he-sends-1-5m-cold-emails-per-month-here-s-what-works-in-2025)
- Scale: 800+ meetings a month across 120+ clients, run by about 10 people. (https://smartlead.ai/blog/case-study-leadbird-by-nick-abraham)

## Clay and Clay University

- Clay 101 teaches FETE: Find, Enrich, Transform, Export. (https://university.clay.com/courses/clay-101)
- Waterfall enrichment lifts coverage from about 30% to 80%+. One five-tool personal email test reached 79%. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)
- Score tier-one accounts on fit, engagement and contract value. Measure meetings booked, hours saved, and trial conversion. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)
- First-party signals (CRM notes, replies, call transcripts) are the moat, and rented signals are not. No blast-all sends. (https://www.clay.com/blog/gtm-engineering)

## Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist benchmarks

- Instantly 2026, covering calendar 2025: average reply 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%+, top 10% at 10.7%+. Below 2% means something is broken. 58% of replies come from step 1. (https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- Instantly: 4-7 steps, under 80 words, follow-ups at 2-3 days then 4-7, warmup 2-4 weeks, bounce under 2%, about $153 per meeting. The same page also calls 3 emails optimal and puts the safe ceiling near 100 sends per warmed inbox per day. (https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- Smartlead Q1-Q3 2025, 3.2M emails: inbox placement about 73% by Q3, with Gmail the best. 50-80 words got a 9.1% reply. Mid-week sends do 22% better. The 3rd follow-up hit 7.4% reply against 3.2% for the 1st. (https://marketing-assets.smartlead.ai/guides/Cold_Email_Blueprint_Q1_Q3_2025.pdf)
- Lemlist's own roundup: Belkins measured 0.45% on 7.5M emails under strict counting. Meeting rate runs 0.5-2.5%. Spam complaints must stay under 0.3%. SaaS often lands under 2% reply. (https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/)
- Lemlist's "17% reply on image-personalized sequences" and "2.8x lift from video" circulate on third-party sites, and I could not trace either to a primary Lemlist source. **Unverified.**

## Jordan Crawford (Blueprint GTM)

- PQS, the pain-qualified segment: public data proves the pain before the first email. Job titles never prove it. The pain should be new, existential, and findable in data. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/, https://cannonballgtm.substack.com/p/the-cannonball-gtm-glossary-essential?r=56vz7i)
- PVP, the permissionless value prop: send output the prospect would pay for before asking for anything. (https://www.joinpavilion.com/blog/the-man-who-mastered-ai-gtm-meet-jordan-crawford)
- Pain sources: live job descriptions, tech installed last week, what a funding release says the money is for, competitor reviews naming missing features, regulatory changes. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)
- 50 companies matching a sharp heuristic beat 5,000 generic ones. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)
- Get to the pain in two sentences, with no scrolling on a phone. Subject lines read like an internal note. CTA is a low-friction question that confirms the pain, never a 15-minute ask. (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jordan-crawford/)

## Kyle Coleman (ex-Looker, Clari, Copy.ai CMO)

- 72 words max in his formula. Hard ceiling of 125 words or one scroll. One idea per line. (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman, https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyletcoleman_cold-email-101-activity-6899031112140554240-4NQd)
- Structure: a trigger as the intro, a body tying the trigger to the persona's outcome, then the CTA. (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman)
- Interest CTAs ("Curious to learn more?") with "Either way, thanks for reading." (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/formula-cold-email-kyle-coleman)
- Never open with "Based on your title," "I saw your profile," or "My company does." (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyletcoleman_sdr-sales-activity-6925131006093975552-Tc-4)

## Grading a campaign before it goes out

Five gates, in the order they run. The rubrics come from Growth Engine X, Instantly, Clay and lastlook; disagreements between them are listed at the end of the file.

**Gate 1, the list.** Score it before writing copy.

- Growth Engine X scores eight dimensions 0-100: verification coverage (100% required, 0 below 50%), duplicate emails (under 1% fine, over 5% a problem), leads per domain (1-2 ideal, 5+ over-indexed, cap at 3), title match to the ICP list (80%+), bad titles such as intern, assistant, coordinator, student, part-time or retired (under 2% normal, over 10% means the filter is loose), catch-all share (under 5%), ICP fit on industry and headcount (80%+), and names that look human (95%+). Verification and ICP fit count double. 90+ ships, 80-89 ships after small fixes, 70-79 fixes the top three issues first, under 60 gets rebuilt. Never upload below B, and skip the scorecard on lists under 100. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/list-quality-scorecard/SKILL.md) Its catch-all examples (info@, contact@) are really role addresses; count role addresses and catch-all domains as two separate numbers.
- Re-run the verifier whenever fixes shrink the list by more than 10%. (same file)
- Instantly scores seven dimensions: verified share 95%+ (weight 3), catch-all under 10% (weight 2), invalid or risky rows left in the send set about 0 (weight 2), duplicates plus blocklist hits under 5%, ICP match 80%+, title relevance, and rows missing first name or company under 10%. Grade C means fix first, D or F means stop. Unverified rows block the send whatever the grade. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/list-quality-scorecard.md)

**Gate 2, the copy.** Growth Engine X scores each email 0-100: situation recognition 25, value clarity with proof 25, personalization quality 20, CTA effort 15, punchiness at 50-90 words 10, subject line 5. 85+ ships, 70-84 gets one more pass, under 70 starts over. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/campaign-copywriting/SKILL.md) Its pass/fail QA list must be all green too: a specific signal in line one, every fact verifiable, no banned phrases, at least 3 recipient sentences per sender sentence, a CTA answerable in five words, under 20 seconds read aloud, no em dashes, and a subject of 2-4 words or the whole offer. (same file)

**Gate 3, the recipient read.** Clay reviews as the specific recipient asking why they wouldn't reply. Check the strategy first (recipient, situation, problem, mechanism, value, offer, ask), then the lines. Raise only objections that would change the recipient's answer. Stop when none remain, or when the fix needs missing proof or a different offer, and list those as Unresolved instead of writing around them. (https://github.com/clay-run/agent-plugins/blob/main/clay/skills/campaigns-sequence-reviewing/SKILL.md)

**Gate 4, the rendered output.** lastlook renders every variant against every real lead and checks the output, since the template hides the bugs. Its 35 checks cover merge failures (blank fields, a literal tag shipping), enrichment leaks ("couldn't find information", N/A mid-sentence), raw CRM values (all-caps names, "Inc." mid-sentence, a full name after Hi), list problems (duplicates, role inboxes, free mail in a B2B list, too many people at one domain), copy hygiene (invisible characters, placeholder text, a previous client's name arriving through the data), and structure (duplicate variants, shared openers, follow-ups that break threading). 19 checks block and 16 warn; a zero-day gap between steps blocks while a three-day gap warns. (https://github.com/hernangimeno/lastlook/blob/main/README.md)

- Measure each merge field's fill rate across the list before writing copy that uses it. (same file)
- Drop leads whose stored employer is a community such as Pavilion or Exit Five, because a line built on it reads as automated. (same file)
- Any check that did not run prints as NOT CHECKED. A skipped rule never counts as a pass. (same file)

**Gate 5, infrastructure and the start.** These run before the first send and again a few hours after it.

- Instantly shows four things before activation: the verified lead count, the sequence (steps, variants, subjects), the daily ramp, and every sending domain with its health verdict. A sender counts as healthy only at health score 80+, 2-3+ weeks of warmup, active status and finished setup. A sender missing from the warmup data counts as cold. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/deliverability.md)
- Growth Engine X's checklist adds: a real person's name as sender, a monitored reply-to, a physical address and an opt-out in every email, the suppression list from prior sends loaded, test sends landing in the primary tab at a personal Gmail and a personal Outlook, every variable resolved on a 5-lead preview, weekdays only in recipient business hours, and bounce-pause and reply-pause on. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/cold-email-starter-kit/references/12-launch-checklist.md)
- Check again 2-4 hours after the start: sent count equals daily limit times active inboxes, bounces under 5%, zero platform errors. Then end of days 1, 2 and 3. (same file)
- Unify's lifecycle runs scaffold, preview, approved, enrolled, and only enrolled sends. Never report anything as sent before that stage, and stop a default brief at previews for review. (https://github.com/unifygtm/agent-plugins/blob/main/unify/skills/outreach/SKILL.md)

## Growth Engine X skill pack (growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills, MIT)

Rules from the agency's public skill pack that the Growth Engine X section above lacks. Grading rubrics are in the grading section.

- Target 50-90 words. Go up to 125 only when AI personalization or creative ideas earn it. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/campaign-copywriting/SKILL.md)
- Personalized subjects are lowercase. Never "Curious" or "Quick question" as a subject. (same file)
- No follow-up opens by pointing at the earlier email ("just checking in", "following up on my last note"). Each one has to stand alone. (same file)
- Rotate the offer across follow-ups through save time, make money and save money. (same file)
- Sequence: day 0; day 3-4 threaded with a blank subject; day 7-8 a new thread with a new subject; day 11-12 a final email that redirects to a named colleague or offers resources. (same file)
- Spintax on subject, first line and CTA, 3-5 spin points per email. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/cold-email-starter-kit/references/12-launch-checklist.md)
- Never close with a line promising to stop on silence ("I'll take silence as a no"), because the sequence keeps sending. Only an explicit opt-out stops it. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/spam-word-checker/SKILL.md)
- Its spam list bans common single words in cold copy, among them sales, marketing, new, now, free trial and invoice, plus greetings before the first name. Hyphenating a banned root (cash-cycle) doesn't make it safe. (same file)
- The 1% rule: a healthy domain replies at 1% or better after 200 sends. Below that on a fair sample, something is broken. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/email-deliverability-audit/SKILL.md)
- Floors before any verdict: 200 sends for reply rate, 50 for bounce rate, 30 classified bounces for bounce composition, 30 days of domain age for any cancel (unknown age counts as young), 100-300 seeds per placement test. Below a floor the answer is "don't know yet". (same file)
- Inbox placement of 85-90% is the minimum for a domain to count as good. Above 90%, send more. (same file)
- Domain verdicts run as a ladder, first match wins: in warmup, excluded; under 30 days, too young; under 200 sends, insufficient data; blocklist share above 20% of 30+ classified bounces, burned at any bounce rate; bounce above 3%, split by sender share (under 25% a list problem, 25-50% both, over 50% check SPF, DKIM and DMARC: missing means fix it, present means burned); reply 1%+, healthy; otherwise run a placement test; placement above 90% means targeting, offer or copy, not the domain; otherwise send the same copy from a known-good control fleet: control fine means burned, both poor means copy. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/inbox-lifecycle-manager/references/decision-ladder.md)
- Look for DKIM at google._domainkey (TXT) and selector1 and selector2._domainkey (CNAME, Microsoft 365) before calling it missing. Checking only `default` flags a healthy fleet. (same file)
- Never cancel a burned domain into a capacity shortfall. Promote an insurance domain that is out of warmup, 30+ days old and at 98%+ reputation, or keep the burned one and buy replacements first. (same file)
- Weekly rhythm: Monday deliverability audit, Wednesday positive-reply sweep, Friday retrospectives, inbox lifecycle every other Monday, a placement test monthly, an experiment review quarterly. Above 2% bounce, triage; above 3%, pause the campaign. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/cold-email-weekly-rhythm/SKILL.md)
- The north-star metric is positive replies over total sent, with OOO and bounces out of the denominator. 1%+ is good and 2%+ great. Hostile replies above 0.3% or unsubscribes above 2% mean pause. Referrals ("not me, talk to X") count as positive. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/positive-reply-scoring/SKILL.md)
- Reply classes: positive interested, positive soft, positive referral, neutral question, not now, not a fit, hostile, unsubscribe, OOO, bounce, other. A label under 0.7 confidence becomes other. (same file)
- Change one variable per experiment, run as two campaigns that split the inboxes and start together, never an in-campaign A/B. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/experiment-design/SKILL.md)
- Under 500 sends per arm can't separate signal from noise. Rough sizes: 1% to 2% positive needs about 500 per arm, 1% to 1.5% about 2,000, 2% to 4% about 250. Read results only after the whole sequence ends, around day 21. (same file)
- Adopt a winner at 20%+ lift with enough sample; replicate on fresh leads at 10-20%; call under 10% inconclusive. Never adopt a winner from a test that changed several things. (same file)
- Test levers in this order: list, offer, subject, opener, CTA, timing, sequence length. (same file)
- Answer positive replies within 30 minutes and offer two specific times. The pack estimates a positive left 24 hours loses about half its booking chance. (https://github.com/growthenginenowoslawski/coldoutboundskills/blob/main/skills/cold-email-starter-kit/references/10-reply-handling.md)
- Never answer an OOO. Move the lead's next send to the return date plus one day. (same file)
- A negative gets one short acknowledgment and the lead leaves the sequence. Never argue or counter-pitch, and honor unsubscribes the same day. (same file)
- Every Monday, hand-check 10-20 AI-categorized replies; the pack puts platform categorization at about 90% accurate. (same file)

## ColdIQ skill repo (Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills, no license file)

Paraphrased only. The repo routes data work through ColdIQ's paid marketplace, so its tool advice is a sales funnel; the rules below are the parts that stand without it. The trigger library is its own section below.

- Four outreach categories by whether the prospect knows you: inbound (hand-raisers), postbound (engaged but not asking), bridgebound (an action unrelated to your marketing), outbound (cold). Inbound copy is the trigger in line one and a booking CTA in line two. Pure cold needs 1:1 research and a pattern-interrupt first line. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/master-skills/signal-sourcer/resources/signal-taxonomy.md)
- Signal points: demo or pricing request 100, 3+ pricing-page visits in 7 days 80, champion moved to a target account 75, several stakeholders from one account 70, trial signup 65, G2 comparison 60, Series A-C round 45, relevant job post 40, intent surge 70+ 40, webinar 25, single site visit 10, email open 5. Recency multiplies: 1.5x inside 24 hours, 1.2x inside 7 days, 0.7x at 30 days, 0.3x past 30. Act at 150+ by hand the same hour, 100-149 within 24 hours, 50-99 into nurture. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/master-skills/signal-sourcer/resources/signal-scoring.md)
- Length by seniority: managers and ICs 3-4 sentences and 90 words max; VPs and C-level 2-3 sentences and 60 words max. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/email-metrics-benchmarks/SKILL.md)
- Email shape: trigger opening, an assumption about their situation, social proof with a number, an open question. (same file)
- Diagnose by symptom: opens under 30% means subject or deliverability; opens without replies means relevance; replies that book under 20% of the time mean the CTA or the qualification. (same file)
- 60-90 words, no bullets, one CTA. The repo allows eight frameworks: before/after, pattern interrupt, ask before pitch, upfront value, do the math, challenge of similar companies, neutral insight, typical problems by role. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/sdr-outbound-rules/SKILL.md)
- Warmup: weeks 1-2 warmup only; cold starts in week 3 at 5-10 a day per mailbox and reaches 30-50 by week 9. Four weeks minimum before the first cold email (2 aging, 2 warming), 6-8 ideal. Keep warmup running at 10-20 a day forever. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/master-skills/cold-email/resources/frameworks/deliverability-guide.md)
- 2-5 sending domains per SDR and 2-3 mailboxes per domain, never the primary domain. DMARC at p=none for weeks 1-2, quarantine at pct=50 for weeks 3-4, reject from week 5. SPF breaks past 10 DNS lookups. (same file)
- Randomize 30-120 seconds between sends; peak windows are 8-10 AM and 1-3 PM, Tuesday to Thursday, recipient time. (same file)
- Reply rate is the strongest reputation signal: under 3% degrades placement, 5% is the floor for sustained sending. (same file)
- Retry soft bounces 2-3 times over 24-72 hours; remove hard bounces at once. (same file)
- Verify 100% of a list before any send and re-verify anything older than 30 days. Drop unknown and catch-all results, role addresses and free mail. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/master-skills/list-building/resources/data-validation.md)
- B2B data decays about 2.1% a month (about 25% a year), emails 22-30% a year and titles 30-35%. Each quarter, re-verify and drop contacts with no engagement in 90 days. Unsubscribes of 0.3-0.5% are normal; above 1% is a targeting problem. (same file)
- Claimed reply rates: cold with no signal 6-8%, one signal 18-22%, three stacked signals 35-40%, website-visitor follow-up 25-30%, champion job change 20-25%. No method given. **Unverified**, and the same file's other table puts the cold baseline at 0.5-1%. (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/email-metrics-benchmarks/SKILL.md)

## Trigger library (ColdIQ's 137, the strongest 40)

The repo credits its 137 triggers to the Flip The Script framework: 30 inbound, 101 bridgebound across relationship (39), history (16), in-market (20), symptoms (11) and firmographic (15), and 6 outbound. Most entries in the repo are a label only. The angle and timing below come from the repo's templates and timing tables, paraphrased; where the repo gives no angle, the line says so. Format: trigger, what to say, when.

Inbound (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/inbound-triggers-30/SKILL.md, timing from https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/master-skills/signal-sourcer/resources/signal-scoring.md):

1. Demo request: personal reply plus a calendar link, under 5 minutes.
2. 3+ pricing-page visits by a VP or above: name the pain the pricing page implies, within 1 hour.
3. G2 or TrustRadius comparison activity: send a competitive one-pager, within 24 hours.
4. Webinar attendee: the recording plus one related offer, within 72 hours.
5. Free-trial or freemium signup: the trigger alone in line one, a booking CTA in line two.
6. New follower of the company page: filter to ICP, reach out within 24-48 hours (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/gtm-plays-11/SKILL.md).
7. Intent-data surge (Bombora 70+): reference the research topic, within 24 hours.

Relationship (https://github.com/Cold-IQ/ColdIQ-s-GTM-Skills/blob/main/skills/bridgebound-relationship-39/SKILL.md):

8. Shares a VC with you or with a customer: open on the common investor.
9. Referral from a happy customer: name the customer who sent you.
10. Mutual connection with the decision maker: ask for the warm intro before any cold email.
11. Past employee of the prospect company now in your network: ask them who owns the problem.
12. Former employer of one of your employees: send from or cite that employee.
13. Engaged with your founder's or an employee's posts: reference the post they engaged with.
14. Met at an event: name the event.

## Instantly agent skill (Instantly-ai/instantly-skills, MIT)

- Three sequence shapes: intro, bump, breakup at delays of 3 and 3-4 days; a 4-step ladder of problem, proof, new angle, breakup; or trigger-led when the list came from a signal filter. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/sequence-playbook.md)
- Personalization ranks signal, then role pain, then company context, then generic but relevant. (same file)
- The cold CTA ladder asks for interest in emails 1-2, offers the asset in email 3, shows the first calendar link in email 4, and ends on a binary break-up in email 5. Cap at about 5. (same file)
- Subjects under about 60 characters and lowercase. (same file)
- Product proof comes later in the sequence as a pattern seen across teams. Never claim a named company as a customer and never name a competitor. (same file)
- Young domains send 20-30 cold emails per sender a day with slow ramp on. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/deliverability.md)
- Only Verified rows enter the send set. Invalid, risky, catch-all and job-change rows stay out. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/enrichment-and-verify.md)
- Mailboxes needed equals daily target divided by 30, rounded up. Google allows up to 5 mailboxes a domain and Microsoft up to 50 a new domain, per Instantly's done-for-you API. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/scale-senders.md)
- Reply statuses: interested, meeting booked, meeting completed, won, OOO, not interested, wrong person, lost, no-show. (https://github.com/Instantly-ai/instantly-skills/blob/main/skills/instantly-gtm/references/reply-triage.md)
- Tag objections by sub-type, each with one move: price gets an outcome anchor (no discount), timing gets a dated check-back, not the decision maker gets a referral ask, already has a tool gets one wedge and then silence, send me info gets one line plus the booking link. (same file)
- Short, ambiguous or forwarded replies go to a "you look" pile for a human. Never guess a label. (same file)

## Gooseworks skills (gooseworks-ai/goose-skills, MIT)

## Explorium skills (explorium-ai/gtm-skills, MIT)

## Where they agree

- Short copy: 50-100 words, with Coleman at 72 and Braun and Instantly under 80.
- Plain text, a conversational tone, and no links or tracking in email 1.
- A soft, interest-based, or offer CTA beats asking for a meeting.
- Narrow, signal- or pain-defined segments beat big lists.
- Bounces under 2-3%, rotated domains, backup inboxes, and a warmup of 2 to 6 weeks.
- Measure positive replies and meetings, not opens.
- Personalize with one relevant variable. A whole AI-written email or a flattering first line doesn't count.
- Verify every address before the first send, and never send to rows a verifier marked invalid (Growth Engine X, Instantly, ColdIQ).
- Render and read a handful of real leads (3-10) before the full upload: Growth Engine X previews 5, Explorium runs 5, Gooseworks drafts 3-5, Unify stops at previews, and lastlook renders them all.
- One CTA per email, answerable in a line or five words.

## Where they disagree

- **Sends per inbox per day:** Lieben caps at 30 and the general consensus is 25-30. Instantly's page says about 100 is safe.
- **Sequence length:** Lieben runs 3 steps. Instantly and Braun say 4-7, while Instantly's same page also calls 3 optimal.
- **Where replies come from:** Instantly puts 58% on step 1. Smartlead shows the 3rd follow-up at more than double step 1's reply rate.
- **List size:** Crawford says 50 companies. Lieben says 500-1,000. Nowoslawski kills a campaign only after 500-1,000 sends and wants a TAM over 100k.
- **What a "good" reply rate is:** Abraham 2-4%, Instantly average 3.43%, Belkins 0.45%, Lieben 20-30%. Each counts differently.
- **Personalization:** Nowoslawski puts offer above personalization. Lemlist's unverified figures say heavy visual personalization wins. Crawford calls AI first lines a party trick.
- **Channel:** ColdIQ warms accounts with ads and LinkedIn first. Leadbird and Growth Engine X are email-volume shops.
- **Sends per inbox, from the 2026-10-09 repos:** ColdIQ's own repo runs 30-50 per mailbox a day once warm, against Lieben's public cap of 30. Growth Engine X says 30 and never more. Instantly holds young domains at 20-30.
- **Sequence length, from the repos:** Growth Engine X runs 4 emails (days 0, 3-4, 7-8, 11-12). ColdIQ's repo runs 4 (days 0, 3, 14, then a break-up) even though Lieben posts 3. Instantly defaults to 3 and caps the CTA ladder at about 5. Unify runs 2-4 steps across channels and Gooseworks 3-4.
- **Spintax:** Growth Engine X wants 3-5 spin points per email. Instantly leaves spintax out of its first version. Clay allows it only for wording that doesn't depend on lead facts.
- **Greetings and casual subjects:** Growth Engine X bans "Hi" or "Hey" before the first name and "quick" subjects. Instantly's own worked example opens "Hi {{firstName}}" under the subject "{{firstName}} - quick one".
- **Catch-all addresses:** Growth Engine X tolerates under 5% and Instantly's scorecard under 10%, yet Instantly's verify gate sends Verified rows only and ColdIQ drops catch-alls outright.
- **When a number means anything:** Growth Engine X wants 200 sends before judging a reply rate and 500 per arm for an experiment. Gooseworks gives a variant 100 sends. Amplemarket judges a sequence at 50 and deliverability at 100. Nowoslawski's public talk (above) kills a campaign at 500-1,000.
- **Where to pause on bounces:** Growth Engine X pauses above 3%. ColdIQ pauses at 3-5% and stops above 5%. Amplemarket calls 2-5% a warning and only above 5% critical.
- **Length by seniority:** ColdIQ caps VPs and C-level at 60 words. Growth Engine X targets 50-90 for everyone. Gooseworks says under 150 with most at 80-120 in one skill, and 50-90 for touch 1 in another. Explorium's template allows 4-6 sentences.
- **The calendar ask:** Explorium's sample email ends on a 15-minute call this week. Growth Engine X bans exactly that ask. Instantly holds the first calendar link until email 4.
- **Champion job change timing:** ColdIQ's scoring file says reach out within 24 hours, while two other ColdIQ files and Gooseworks' structure say days 14-45 in the new seat or day 1 of a 14-day sequence.
- **Funding timing:** ColdIQ's trigger file says 2-4 weeks after the announcement. Its scoring file says within 24 hours for $10M+ rounds. Amplemarket calls the last 7 days the hottest.
- **Speed to a reply:** Growth Engine X says 30 minutes for a positive reply. ColdIQ says under 5 minutes for an inbound demo request.
- **ColdIQ against itself:** the same benchmarks file puts cold reply rates at 6-8% in one table and 0.5-1% in another.
