## 1. CONTRADICTIONS

**60-30-10, what the 60 is.** Showalter says the 60 must be neutral and the brand color drops to 30: "we want to make sure that we're using 60% of our neutral or our base color" (UWwNIMHFdW4, 1:31), with the brand as "some sort of primary color... We want that to be 30%" (2:06). Flux Academy runs the brand color at 60: "the 60% is probably what's going to be your background, and then 30% is going to be kind of main elements" (KMS3VwGh3HY, 4:19), and their worked example is a purple hero at 60% with white at 30%. A linter cannot implement both. Showalter partly concedes the inversion at 4:47 ("your bright color is 60%... that's okay, I'm down with that"), so treat neutral-60 as the default and brand-60 as the sanctioned exception.

**Color format.** UX Tools: "when making a color palette you should use HSB instead of hex or RGB" (yYwEnLYT55c, 2:37). Sajid rules HSB's family out: "With HSL, the biggest issue is how it handles lightness. The dark and light shades lose all saturation. With LCH and OK LCH, the increments look way more natural" (vvPklRN0Tco, 8:26). HSB brightness has the same failure mode HSL lightness does, and UX Tools' entire 90-100-saturation/20-30-brightness recipe (5:18) exists to hand-correct exactly that defect. Both agree hex and RGB are wrong. UX Tools also states the hue range wrong: "It's a number from 1 to 365" (2:55) against Sajid's correct "0 to 360" (1:00).

**How many greys and how many shades.** UX Tools: nine shades per color, 100 to 900, "Try to stick to a maximum of 10 shades" (7:40), across brand plus four supporting plus neutrals, so roughly 54 declared values. Sajid ships three background lightnesses and two text lightnesses: "0% lightness for our base color, then 5%... and finally 10%" (1:58). Five greys against nine to ten. Sajid's whole framing is "colors are honestly the easiest part of the UI" (0:00); UX Tools' is a five-step process.

**Palette size.** Flux: three colors total, "add two more colors so that you can have a palette of three colors" (KMS3VwGh3HY, 1:55). UX Tools requires four supporting colors on top of brand and neutrals (yYwEnLYT55c, 3:26). A three-colour palette cannot carry success, warning, danger and info.

**x-height, inside one video.** Mizko sets the bar at "68 to 69% range, which is what is preferred" (mEAmAFgzQd4, 1:10) and restates it as "around 68 to 70%" (3:35), then recommends six fonts outside it: Work Sans 75%, DM Sans 72%, Switzer 79%, Open Sans 78%, Satoshi 66%, Supreme 67%. Only Inter (69%) and Space Grotesk (70%) satisfy his own rule.

**Grid discipline.** Kole Jain: "Grids and layouts are also much more strictly followed for dashboards than landing pages" (B7k5rOgmOGY, 2:10). Aliena Cai: "don't let the grid constrain your design it is okay to not follow the grid sometimes" (shuIfhrLIP0, 10:15). Different contexts, but a linter needs the split written down.

**Verification.** The taste skill's selling point is that "Each skill has its own process for verifying that it doesn't look like AI slop before it returns it to you" (5MJ7hzwjRbo, 2:58). Eight minutes later the same reviewer removes verification: "I actually put this note inside of my Claude MD. I said, 'Don't over-verify. The user handles visual checking'" (12:25).

## 2. HOW TASTE IS ACQUIRED

**Screenshot directory, daily, categorized.** Arora's single most concrete mechanism. "I'll just take a screenshot, add it to my screenshots folder, and I'll do this every day" (g5R67TAhpbU, 14:26), reaching "1000s of images", then indexed because the volume broke flat browsing: "This was organized by categories" (15:16). The claimed effect is subconscious: "What you're actually doing is you're internalizing... You're training like that" (16:15).

**Replicate one site end to end, locally, and never ship it.** "If you want to acquire design, taste of linear, you will have to replicate this entire landing page top to bottom with the last minute details of even the hover effects" (18:32), and "Can you replicate or shamelessly mercilessly copy? Can you replicate? Do not publish it" (19:55). The unit is a whole page, not a component.

**Study few people deeply.** "Study few people's deeply. Not many shallowly" (18:18).

**Recreate a screenshot inside a foreign design system.** Cai's exercise: "take a screenshot of another app or website that you like and try to recreate that page by using this ant design system" (shuIfhrLIP0, 14:26). Same replication mechanism as Arora's, constrained by a token set, which isolates layout judgment from styling.

**Adopt someone else's tokens so their taste rides along.** "if you're using tailwind, you are inherently using a design system, inherently using someone else's face onto your own code base. So you acquire that as well" (g5R67TAhpbU, 17:40).

**Solicit brutal feedback and detach first.** "Give me brutal feedback. I won't take it to my heart... First, detach yourself from the design" (20:07). Arora names the failure mode directly: "people generally once you ask for feedback, they give generic feedback. They don't want to come out as a critique" (3:30).

**The mismatch test.** RISD's version, the only falsifiable taste protocol in the batch: show thumbnails with no explanation, "i just say what do you see if what they see matches what i want the audiences to see i know i've done a good job but if they see something completely different i know i need to go back and revise" (aZJDdfJHKxM, 1:51).

**Mid-process critique.** "if a student is going down the wrong track or making a decision that is going to perceive wrong later in the future you can catch that in the middle" (aZJDdfJHKxM, 2:25).

**Three-stage ladder, with a diagnostic at each rung.** Arora: "First stage is you don't know what's bad" (5:22); "Second step, you see the problem, but you can't fix it" (6:33); "Third is when you just feel it" (7:58). Rung one is self-diagnosable against a named URL: "if you don't see anything wrong with this, this is where things go wrong."

**Active judgment, not exposure.** ABNUX: "taste is not something that is built passively it's active involvement right... you need to make observations you need to make judgments" (9XQOQ2oTanc, 20:51).
