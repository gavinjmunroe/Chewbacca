## Executive Summary

Tizzy's go-to-market strategy is deliberately phased to build trust, validate outcomes, and scale distribution channels methodically. **The autism wearable market has no established category leader, which means we define the category or die trying.** The GTM strategy prioritizes:

1. **Proof of value first** (adult pilot → measurable outcomes)
2. **Clinical credibility second** (therapy partnerships → institutional trust)
3. **Parent adoption third** (word-of-mouth → mainstream autism market)
4. **Institutional scale fourth** (schools → policy and procurement)

This approach minimizes risk, builds defensible positioning, and avoids the "creepy tech" backlash that killed similar products.

## Market Context: Opportunity and Landmines

### The Opportunity

**Autism Prevalence (2026):**
- 1 in 36 children in the U.S. diagnosed with autism (CDC)
- ~2.3M autistic youth under 18 in the U.S.
- Growing adult diagnosis rates (self-recognition, better screening)

**Conversational Challenges:**
- Social communication difficulties are core to autism diagnosis (DSM-5)
- Many autistic people report "knowing something is wrong but not what"
- Parents cannot observe school/peer conversations
- Speech therapy often fails to generalize from clinic to real world

**Existing Solutions are Inadequate:**
- In-person speech therapy: expensive, limited hours, clinic-only context
- Social skills groups: artificial settings, poor real-world transfer
- Apps and games: no real-world feedback loop, engagement drops fast
- Nothing captures and coaches actual conversations

**Market Timing:**
- Voice AI maturity (Modulate ELM, prosody understanding beyond transcripts)
- Wearable form factor validation (Ray-Ban Meta, Apple Watch, Friend pendant)
- Autism self-advocacy movement (demand for assistive tech, not "cure" narratives)

### The Landmines

**"Creepy Tech" Backlash:**
- Friend pendant faced bystander suspicion ("wearing a wire")
- Always-listening devices trigger privacy fears
- Covert recording is socially and legally toxic

**Regulatory Complexity:**
- COPPA for minors under 13
- State recording consent laws (one-party vs. all-party)
- FERPA for schools
- FTC health data breach notification

**Autism Community Sensitivity:**
- Strong backlash against "fix autism" narratives
- Suspicion of tech that teaches masking (hiding autistic identity)
- Demand for co-design, not top-down solutions

**Hardware Risk:**
- Battery life, audio quality, durability
- Supply chain and manufacturing delays
- High upfront capital requirements

## GTM Wave 1: College Transition Pilot (Months 0-6)

### Target Segment

**Autistic college students (ages 18-22) navigating new social environments.**

**Why This Wedge:**
- Consent is simple (no parental approval needed, no COPPA)
- High pain point (new environment, social overload, adjustment struggles)
- Motivated users (want to improve, open to tech solutions)
- Fast feedback (30-90 day cycles, measurable outcomes)
- Strong word-of-mouth potential (tight communities, online forums)

**Size of Addressable Market:**
- ~150,000 autistic college students in U.S. (rough estimate)
- 30-40% face social adjustment challenges (literature-based)
- **TAM for pilot: ~50,000 high-need students**

### Go-To-Market Tactics

**Recruitment Channels:**
1. **College Disability Services Offices**
   - Direct outreach to DSO directors
   - Position as research pilot/early access program
   - Offer free devices + premium features

2. **Autism Support Programs on Campus**
   - Many universities have autism-specific transition programs
   - Partner with program directors for cohort recruitment

3. **Online Communities**
   - Reddit (r/autism, r/AutismTranslated, r/college)
   - Autistic Discord servers and online forums
   - TikTok and YouTube (autistic creators, #ActuallyAutistic)

4. **Autism Advocacy Organizations**
   - ASAN (Autistic Self Advocacy Network)
   - College Autism Network
   - Local/regional chapters

**Marketing Message:**
- "Starting college is overwhelming. Tizzy helps you navigate conversations when everything feels too much."
- "See patterns you can't see. Get coaching that respects who you are."
- "30-day trial. Free device. No strings attached."

**Pricing for Pilot:**
- **Free device loan** for 50-100 beta users
- Requires 30-day commitment + weekly check-ins
- Optional: $50/week participation incentive for data donation

### Success Metrics (Wave 1)

**Recruitment:**
- 50-100 participants enrolled in 3 months
- 70% retention through 30 days
- 80% completion of weekly surveys

**Product Validation:**
- Daily active usage >60% (wear device 5+ days/week)
- Post-conversation review engagement >40%
- Haptic coaching perceived as helpful (4/5 or higher)

**Outcome Validation:**
- 60% show measurable improvement on 1+ target skills (self-report + objective metrics)
- Conversation confidence increases (pre/post survey)
- Social crashes/breakdowns decrease (self-report)

**Word-of-Mouth:**
- NPS score 40+ (would recommend to autistic peers)
- 20% refer at least one friend
- Organic social media mentions/reviews

**Timeline:** Months 0-6
**Budget:** $50,000 (devices, incentives, platform costs)

## GTM Wave 2: Speech-Language & Therapy Channel (Months 6-12)

### Target Segment

**Speech-language pathologists (SLPs) and autism therapy clinics serving ages 8-22.**

**Why This Wedge:**
- SLPs are trusted authorities for parents (credibility unlock)
- Therapy effectiveness is limited by lack of real-world data
- Clinics are actively looking for tools to extend therapy impact
- Validates clinical outcomes (essential for later parent sales)

**Size of Addressable Market:**
- ~150,000 SLPs in U.S. who work with autism (ASHA)
- ~500,000 autistic youth receiving speech/language services
- **TAM for Wave 2: 50,000-100,000 therapy-connected families**

### Go-To-Market Tactics

**Partnership Model:**
1. **Clinic Partnerships (B2B2C)**
   - Tizzy provides devices at cost or subsidized
   - Clinics recruit clients into pilot program
   - Therapists get dashboard access to real-world patterns
   - Shared research collaboration (case studies, publications)

2. **SLP Ambassador Program**
   - Recruit 20-50 influential SLPs as early adopters
   - Provide training on Tizzy coaching framework
   - Co-create content (webinars, case studies, conference talks)
   - Referral incentives for patient recommendations

3. **Professional Association Engagement**
   - Present at ASHA conference (American Speech-Language-Hearing Association)
   - Publish case studies in SLP journals
   - Offer CEU-eligible training on using Tizzy

**Value Proposition for Clinics:**
- "Finally see what happens outside your office—real-world topic maintenance, repair, reciprocity."
- "Turn 1 hour of therapy into 24/7 coaching."
- "Measurable outcomes, publishable research, differentiation from competitors."

**Pricing for Therapy Channel:**
- **Clinic licensing:** $500-1,000/year per therapist (dashboard access, training, support)
- **Device pricing:** $200-300 per device (near cost, subsidized by clinic or parent)
- **Revenue share model (optional):** Clinic recommends, Tizzy sells direct, clinic gets 10-20% referral fee

### Success Metrics (Wave 2)

**Partnership Acquisition:**
- 10-20 clinic partnerships signed in 6 months
- 50+ SLPs trained and actively using Tizzy

**Patient Adoption:**
- 200-500 patients enrolled through therapy channel
- 60% retention through 90 days
- 80% therapist satisfaction (dashboard, insights, outcomes)

**Clinical Validation:**
- 60% show measurable improvement on 2+ ASHA benchmark skills
- Therapist-validated outcome reports for 100+ patients
- 1-2 published case studies or pilot research papers

**Revenue Validation:**
- $50,000-100,000 in therapy channel revenue
- Proof that clinics will pay for licenses
- Proof that parents will buy on therapist recommendation

**Timeline:** Months 6-12
**Budget:** $100,000 (devices, clinic subsidies, training materials, partnership development)

## Competitive Positioning

### Direct Competitors (None Yet)

**As of Feb 2026, there is no direct "autism conversation coaching wearable" on the market.**

Closest adjacent products:
- Friend pendant (AI companion, not coaching—backlash risk)
- Limitless Pendant (memory/transcription, not autism-specific)
- Speech therapy apps (Otsimo, Proloquo2Go—not real-world capture)

**Tizzy's Defensible Position:**
- First-mover in autism-specific conversational coaching
- Dataset moat (no one else has autism conversation data at scale)
- Clinical credibility (therapy partnerships, outcome validation)
- Privacy-first positioning (vs. surveillance/creepy competitors)

### Indirect Competitors

**In-person speech therapy:**
- Expensive ($100-200/session)
- Limited hours (1-2 hours/week)
- Clinic-only context (poor real-world generalization)
- **Tizzy is complementary, not replacement**

**Social skills apps:**
- Gamified, not real-world
- Poor engagement/retention
- No feedback loop
- **Tizzy delivers what apps promise but can't deliver**

**Wearables (Apple Watch, etc.):**
- General-purpose, not autism-specific
- No conversation coaching
- **Tizzy could integrate with Apple Watch later (sensor fusion)**

## Risk Mitigation: Avoiding the "Creepy Tech" Death Spiral

### Why Friend Pendant Failed Socially

- Always listening, no clear utility
- Bystanders felt surveilled ("wearing a wire")
- No visible consent mechanism
- Positioned as companionship (AI friend), not assistive tech

### How Tizzy Avoids This

1. **Clear Assistive Tech Positioning**
   - "Training wheels for conversation," not surveillance
   - Medical/therapeutic framing (legitimate mission)

2. **Visible Legitimacy**
   - Device design suggests premium assistive tech (like hearing aids)
   - Therapist endorsements and partnerships
   - Clinical validation and published research

3. **Consent-Forward**
   - Standardized consent mechanism (social script, indicator)
   - Bystander protection (focus on wearer behavior)
   - Transparent data governance

4. **Value is Obvious**
   - Measurable outcomes (not vague "companionship")
   - 30-day proof of value
   - Testimonials from users and parents

5. **Community Co-Design**
   - Built with autistic input, not just for autistics
   - Advisory board of autistic self-advocates
   - Open feedback loops and product governance
